Nile Valley Wisdom

The Ancient Roots of the Covenant — Egypt, the Source of All

△ △ △

Before the scriptures were written, before Moshe descended Sinai, before Yeshua walked the waters of Galilee — there was Kemet. From the banks of the sacred Nile arose the first words of Jah spoken to humanity: the Hymn to Aten, the 42 Declarations of Maat, and the Hermetic wisdom of Poimandres. These are the roots of all covenant theology — restored here to their rightful place at the beginning of all things.

☀ The Great Hymn to Aten Composed by Pharaoh Akhenaten · Kemet · c. 1346 BCE

How manifold are thy works! They are hidden from before us, O thou sole Jah, whose powers no other possesseth. Thou didst create the earth according to thy heart while thou wast alone — all men, all cattle great and small, all that are upon the earth that walk on their feet, all that soar above and fly with their wings.

The foreign countries of Syria and Kush, and the land of Egypt — thou placest every man in his place and providest their daily bread. Every man has his food, and his time of life is reckoned. Their tongues are diverse in speech, their forms likewise and their skins — for thou hast differentiated the nations.

Thou makest the Nile in the Underworld, and thou bringest it forth as thou desirest to sustain the people, even as thou hast made them for thyself, their Lord in their entirety, who art weary on their behalf, Lord of every land, who risest for them, thou Aten of the day, great in thy glory.

The foreign lands also, thou hast made their life — thou hast set a Nile in the sky so that it may come down for them and make floods upon the mountains like the great sea, watering their fields in their towns. How beautiful are thy designs, thou Lord of Eternity!

The Nile in heaven — it is for the strangers and for the herds of every land that walk on their feet. But the Nile — it cometh from the Underworld for Egypt.

Thy beams embrace the lands to the fullness of all that thou hast made. Thou art Ra, and thou reachest to their ends and subduest them for thy beloved son. Though thou art afar, thy beams are upon the earth; though thou art on high, thy footprints are the day.

When thou settest in the western horizon of the sky, the earth is in darkness like death. They sleep in their chambers, their heads are wrapped up, no eye can see its fellow. Were all their possessions stolen which are under their heads, they would not know it. Every lion cometh forth from his den, all worms that bite, the darkness is their nest. The earth is in silence, for he who made them hath set in his horizon.

The earth is bright when thou risest in the horizon, when thou shinest as Aten in the daytime. Thou drivest away the darkness and givest thy rays. The Two Lands keep festival, awakened and standing on their feet when thou hast raised them up. They wash their bodies, they take their garments, their arms are uplifted in adoration of thy dawning.

All the world does its work. All cattle rest upon their pasturage, the trees and the plants flourish. The birds flutter in their marshes, their wings uplifted in adoration to thee. All the sheep dance upon their feet, all winged things fly — they live when thou hast shone upon them.

Thou art in my heart, and there is none other that knoweth thee save thy son Akhenaten. Thou hast made him wise in thy designs and in thy might. The earth came into being by thy hand, as thou hast made them. When thou risest, they live; when thou settest, they die. For thou art Duration thyself, and man liveth by thee.

Eyes feast on beauty until thou settest. All works are laid aside when thou settest in the west. When thou risest, they are made to grow for the King. Haste is made in every leg since thou didst found the earth, and thou dost raise them up for thy son who came forth from thee — the King of Upper and Lower Egypt.

⚖ The 42 Declarations of Maat The Negative Confession · Book of Coming Forth by Day · Kemet

These are the declarations made before the 42 Assessors of Maat in the Hall of Two Truths, where the heart is weighed against the feather of righteousness. The soul speaks these words upon entering the presence of Jah:

1. I have not committed sin against men.
2. I have not opposed my family and kinsfolk.
3. I have not acted fraudulently in the Seat of Truth.
4. I have not known men who were of no account.
5. I have not committed any evil act.
6. I have not made the first-fruits of my daily work to fail.
7. I have not done that which is held in abomination by Jah.
8. I have not defamed a servant to his master.
9. I have not caused pain to any man.
10. I have not caused any man to hunger.
11. I have not made any man to weep.
12. I have not committed murder.
13. I have not given the order for murder to be committed.
14. I have not caused calamity to men.
15. I have not plundered the offerings in the temples.
16. I have not purloined the cakes of Jah.
17. I have not carried away the offerings made to the Blessed Dead.
18. I have not fornicated and have not committed sodomy.
19. I have not made light the bushel.
20. I have not filched land from my neighbour's estate and have not added to the weight of the balance to cheat.
21. I have not taken milk from the mouths of children.
22. I have not driven the cattle away from their pasturage.
23. I have not snared the geese of the pens of Jah.
24. I have not caught fish with bait made from the bodies of the same kind of fish.
25. I have not stopped the water when it should flow.
26. I have not cut the dam of a canal.
27. I have not extinguished a flame when it should burn.
28. I have not violated the days of the meat offerings.
29. I have not driven away cattle from the property of Jah.
30. I have not turned back water at its springtide.
31. I have not added to nor have I filched from the weights of the scales.
32. I have not smitten the image of Jah.
33. I have not been extravagant.
34. I have not raked over much; I have not done more than was needful.
35. My mouth is pure; I am without iniquity.
36. I have not wasted the god's offerings.
37. I have not carried away food from the child, nor have I treated with contempt Jah of my city.
38. I have not slain the cattle belonging to Jah.
39. I have not acted with deceit.
40. I have not stolen the bread of Jah.
41. I have not carried away the khenfu cakes from the Spirits of the dead.
42. I have not snatched away the bread of the child, nor treated with contempt Jah of my town. I have not slain the cattle sacred to Jah. I am pure. I am pure. I am pure. I am pure.

🜁 Poimandres The Vision of Hermes Trismegistus · The Corpus Hermeticum, Book I

1Once, when thought came to me of the things that are, and my understanding was raised to a great height, while my bodily senses were withdrawn as in sleep, methought a being more than vast, of infinite greatness, called my name and said to me:

2What wouldst thou hear and see, and what dost thou wish to learn and understand from thy thought?

3I said: Who art thou? — I am Poimandres, the Mind of the Sovereignty. I know what thou desirest, and I am with thee everywhere.

4I said: I long to learn the things that are, and understand their nature, and know Jah. This is what I long to hear. — I have it in my mind to be with thee, for I am Mind.

5Even as he spake, forthwith all things changed in aspect before me, and were opened out in a moment. And I beheld a limitless view; everything became light — sweet, joyous light. And I became enchanted at the sight.

6But in a little while there had come to be in one part a downward-tending darkness, terrible and grim, coiling in sinuous folds, so that methought it like unto a serpent. Then the darkness changed into some sort of humid nature, tossed about beyond all power of words, belching out smoke as from a fire, and giving forth a wailing sound that beggars all description.

7And thereafter there was hurled upward out of it an inarticulate cry; and thereupon from the light there came a holy Word, which took its stand upon the humid Nature; and methought this Word was mightier than the voice of the darkness.

8Hast thou understood what this vision means? — I shall come to know, I said. — That Light is I, even Mind, the first Jah who was before the humid nature which appeared out of darkness; and the Word which came forth from the light is son of Jah.

9— How so? — Thus understand it: that which in thee seeth and heareth is the Word of the Lord; but Mind is the Father Jah. They are not separated one from the other; for life is the union of them.

10— I thank thee. — But why dost thou give thanks, when thou hast not yet seen? When thou hast seen it, thou wilt be filled with beauteous vision. For see, even now a further vision has begun to show itself to me.

11Poimandres spake again to me and said: I have manifested myself to thee in this vision, for I am the Light, even the Mind, the first that was before the humid nature that appeared out of darkness.

12Mind then is Jah, and the Word that came from Mind is Son of Jah, and they two are separate not one from the other, for Life is the union of Word and Mind. Think of this not with the eyes of the body, but with the eyes of understanding.

13Then I said: I fain would learn again of Mind. — Speak on, said he, for I am present with thee as a teacher.

14Jah is not Mind, but the Cause that maketh Mind to be; not Spirit, but the Cause that maketh Spirit to be; not Light, but the Cause that maketh Light to be. Therefore do thou worship Jah, and if thou wouldst worship him, use this approach — through Mind alone.

15He that hath understood himself, goeth unto that Jah. This is the final good of them that have obtained knowledge — to be made Jah. Why then dost thou delay? Must it not be, since thou hast all received, that thou shouldst become a guide to those who are worthy?

16And Poimandres mingled with the Powers, and I gave thanks to the Father of the Universe, and was set free, endued with power, and instructed touching the nature of the universe and the supreme beholding — and I sang praises to the Father of all things and said:

Holy is Jah, the Father of all things.
Holy is Jah, whose counsel is performed by his own Powers.
Holy is Jah, who willeth to be known, and is known by them that are his own.
Holy art thou, who by thy Word hast established all things.
Holy art thou, whose brightness Nature hath not darkened.
Holy art thou, of whom all Nature is the image.
Holy art thou, who art stronger than all things.
Holy art thou, who art better than all praises.
Accept pure offerings of speech from a soul and heart uplifted to thee — thou of whom no words can tell, no tongue can speak, whom silence only can declare.
