The Nag Hammadi Library

Gospel of Truth · Thunder Perfect Mind · Apocryphon of John

In December 1945, an Egyptian farmer named Muhammad Ali al-Samman discovered thirteen leather-bound papyrus codices buried in a sealed clay jar near the town of Nag Hammadi in Upper Egypt. The fifty-two texts inside were Coptic translations of earlier Greek originals, composed between the first and fourth centuries CE. They had been sealed and buried around 390 CE, likely by monks from the nearby Pachomian monastery fleeing the destruction of heretical texts ordered by Bishop Athanasius. Hidden for sixteen centuries, they emerged into a world that was not yet ready for them — and yet they are among the most important spiritual documents ever recovered by human hands.

✦ The Gospel of Truth Valentinus · c. 140 CE · Joy to Those Who Know the Father

The Gospel of Truth is joy for those who have received from the Father of Truth the grace of knowing him, through the power of the Word that came forth from the fullness — the one who is in the thought and the mind of the Father, that is, the one who is addressed as the Saviour, that being the name of the work he is to perform for the redemption of those who were ignorant of the Father, while the name of the gospel is the proclamation of hope, being discovery for those who search for him.

When the totality went about searching for the one from whom they had come forth — and the totality was inside of him, the incomprehensible, inconceivable one who is superior to every thought — ignorance of the Father brought about anguish and terror; and the anguish grew solid like a fog, so that no one was able to see. For this reason error became powerful; it worked on its own matter foolishly, not having known the truth. It set about with a creation, preparing with power and beauty the substitute for the truth.

This is the gospel of the one who is searched for, which was revealed to those who are perfect through the mercies of the Father, the hidden mystery, Yeshua, the Christ. Through this gospel those who are in the dark and forgetfulness were enlightened, through this gospel he enlightened them, gave them a way, and the way is the truth which he taught them.

For this reason, if one has knowledge, he is from above. If he is called, he hears, he answers, and he turns to him who is calling him, and ascends to him. And he knows in what manner he is called. Having knowledge, he does the will of the one who called him, he wishes to be pleasing to him, he receives rest. Each one's name comes to him. He who is to have knowledge in this manner knows where he comes from and where he is going. He knows as one who having become drunk has turned away from his drunkenness, and having returned to himself, has set right what are his own.

He has brought many back from error. He went before them into their places, from which they had moved away, since it was on account of the depth of the one who encircles all spaces while there is none that encircles him. It was a great wonder that they were in the Father without knowing him and that they were able to leave on their own, since they were not able to contain him and know him in whom they were, for indeed his will had not come forth from him.

For the Father is sweet and in his will is what is good. He took cognizance of his own, that they are perfect. He perfected them of himself. The Gospel of Truth is a revelation of hope, and is a guide for those who search — for the perfect belong to the Father and it is to them that the Word came, not that the Word might be explained but that the Word might be experienced in the living silence of the heart.

Say, then, from the heart that you are the perfect day and in you dwells the light that does not fail. Speak of the truth with those who search for it, and of knowledge to those who have committed sin in their error. Make firm the foot of those who have stumbled, and extend your hands to those who are ill. Feed those who are hungry and give repose to those who are weary, and raise up those who wish to rise, and awaken those who sleep. For you are the understanding that is drawn forth. If strength acts thus, it becomes even stronger. Be concerned with yourselves; do not be concerned with other things which you have rejected from yourselves.

Thunder · Perfect Mind

The Voice of the Divine Feminine · The Nag Hammadi Library · Egypt · 4th Century

Found in the sands of Egypt in 1945 buried in a sealed jar — hidden there when the authorities began burning Gnostic texts — Thunder Perfect Mind is one of the most extraordinary documents ever recovered from the ancient world. It speaks in the voice of a divine feminine power announcing herself in paradox, in contradiction, in every category that the human mind uses to divide and separate. She is everything. She contains everything. She will not be defined. She will not be diminished. She speaks like nothing else in the canon of sacred literature. Read it aloud. Read it slowly. Let it land.

✦ What You Just Read · The Teaching Behind the Thunder The Voice of Sophia · The Feminine Face of Jah · The Paradox That Cannot Be Resolved — Only Received

This text was written in Egypt in the early centuries of the Christian era. It was buried in a sealed clay jar near Nag Hammadi in Upper Egypt — hidden when the authorities began burning Gnostic scriptures in the 4th century. It stayed in that jar for sixteen hundred years. In 1945 a farmer digging for fertiliser found it and broke it open. He could not read the Coptic text inside. He brought it home. His mother used some of the pages to light the fire. The rest survived. What you just read survived the fire and the jar and the sixteen centuries because it was meant to be read now. In this generation. By you.

The voice speaking is identified in Gnostic theology as Sophia — Wisdom — the divine feminine presence that exists in the eternal realm as a counterpart to the divine masculine. She is the one who was present at creation — as Proverbs 8 says: 1 was there when He set the heavens in place, 1 was beside Him as a master craftsman. She is the Shekinah of the Kabbalistic tradition — the feminine presence of Jah that dwells in the midst of the people. She is Shakti in the Vedic understanding — the active power, the creative force, the living frequency through which the unmanifest becomes manifest. She is the Mother — the aspect of Jah that the Western church suppressed, the face of the divine that the Ethiopian tradition preserved through its devotion to Mary, its thirty-three Marian feast days, its understanding that Jah is both Father and Mother.

She speaks in paradox because the divine cannot be captured in a single category. The moment you say Jah is only this and not that — only love and not judgment, only gentle and not fierce, only father and not mother — you have reduced the infinite to a concept. Thunder Perfect Mind refuses reduction. She is every opposite simultaneously. She is war and peace. She is knowledge and ignorance. She is first and last. This is not confusion — this is the teaching. The divine holds all polarities in unity. The spiritual path is learning to hold them too — without needing to collapse them into one side or the other.

The Ital transmission in this book speaks of the Anandamaya Kosha — the bliss body — the innermost sheath of the human being that is identical to divine nature. Thunder Perfect Mind is the voice of that innermost reality speaking from within creation. She was buried. She is found. She was scattered. She is gathered. She was hidden from those who looked. She is revealed to those who stop looking and simply receive. Jah Rastafari. The feminine face of the Most High. She was always here.
