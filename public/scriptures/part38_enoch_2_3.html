The Enochic Trilogy

Three Books · One Man · The Watchers · The Seven Heavens · The Throne of Glory · The Face of Jah

Enoch walked with Jah — and was not, for Jah took him. Five words in Genesis 5:24. Five words that contain a entire cosmology. Three books try to tell you where he went.

The Western church removed Enoch. Burned it. Banned it. Called it dangerous. And it was dangerous — because it told the truth about the Watchers, about the fallen ones, about what really happened before the flood. It named the angels. It described the machinery of heaven. It showed Yeshua — the Son of Man — seated on the throne of glory hundreds of years before Yeshua was born in Bethlehem. And that could not be allowed inside a religion that claimed to have invented Yeshua.

The Ethiopian Orthodox Church never removed it. They knew what it was. The Book of Enoch sat in the Ethiopian highlands for two thousand years while the West forgot it existed — then in 1773 a Scottish explorer named James Bruce brought three copies back from Abyssinia and the Western world had to confront what Ethiopia had always known: the Bible was incomplete without Enoch. The Dead Sea Scrolls confirmed it — more copies of Enoch than of any other book except Psalms and Deuteronomy. It was scripture. It was always scripture. Ethiopia knew.

The First Book — 1 Enoch, the Ethiopic Enoch — is canonical. Ethiopian Orthodox. In the Bible. 108 chapters. The Watchers who came to earth and corrupted the bloodlines. The judgment. The cosmic geography of heaven and hell. The Son of Man on the throne. The very imagery that Yeshua used when He called himself Son of Man — He was quoting Enoch. He knew Enoch. He expected His listeners to know Enoch. Now you know.

The Second Book — 2 Enoch, the Slavonic Enoch — takes Enoch through the ten heavens. Level by level. The first heaven where the angels govern the stars. The fifth where fallen angels are kept. The seventh where the elders of Jah stand in radiant silence. The tenth — the face. The face of Jah Himself. No description possible. Only light. Enoch returns to earth, writes everything down in 366 books, and is taken again. A man who saw the face of the Most High and lived to write about it.

The Third Book — 3 Enoch, the Hebrew Enoch, the Sepher Hekhalot — is not in the Ethiopian canon. It is a Sacred Addition. It is the rabbinic tradition of the same Enoch, now transformed into Metatron — the Prince of the Divine Presence, the lesser Jah, the one who sits beside the throne. Rabbi Ishmael ascends through the seven heavenly palaces to find Enoch and ask him everything. Three books. One man. The most extraordinary spiritual biography in the history of the world.
