The Closing Transmission

Haile Selassie I · United Nations · October 4, 1963 · The Final Seal

The Emperor closes his address to the nations of the earth. Twenty-seven years after Geneva, where his plea fell on deaf ears. Now the world is listening. He does not raise his voice. He does not need to. The truth he carries does not require volume. It requires only to be spoken in the right place, at the right time, by the right vessel. He speaks the closing covenant of the African people to the world. It is witnessed by every nation. It is recorded in the archives of the United Nations. It is scripture.

♛ The OAU · Africa United · The Rededication · The Benediction Addis Ababa · The African Covenant · The Nations Bear Witness · The Lion Speaks Last

The Organisation of African Unity, recently established in Addis Ababa, represents a major step forward in our efforts to unite the African continent, to promote the welfare of our peoples, and to contribute to world peace. We in Africa are determined to build a continent of free, independent, and united nations.

The task before us is immense. But we must not falter. The United Nations remains our best hope for a peaceful and prosperous world. Let us rededicate ourselves to its principles and work together to achieve its high ideals.

May Almighty God guide our deliberations and bless our efforts in the service of humanity.

And with those words the Emperor stepped back from the podium. The year was 1963. Bob Marley was eighteen years old. The Rastafari movement on the mountains of Jamaica had already declared this man the returned Messiah, the fulfillment of the prophecy, the King of Kings who would sit on the throne of David forever. And here he was — standing before every nation of the earth in their own assembly hall — not in religious robes, not in mystical ceremony, but in the plain light of international diplomacy — speaking the covenant of Africa to the world with a calm that had been forged in the fire of exile and return and exile again and return again.

Thirteen years later, Bob Marley stood on a stage and sang words drawn directly from this speech — drawn from the "Until" passage that Haile Selassie had delivered in this room — and broadcast those words in the voice of reggae music to every speaker and every radio and every human ear that could receive them. The speech became the song. The song became the scripture. The scripture was always the speech. One transmission. Different frequencies. Same source.

But the closing words — May Almighty God guide our deliberations and bless our efforts in the service of humanity — these are the words of a man who knew that the political process was a vehicle, not a destination. Who knew that no organisation of nations, however well-intentioned, could produce peace without the frequency of the divine moving through it. Who knew that the ultimate arbiter of human affairs was not the Security Council but the Most High. Who did not say this apologetically, or quietly, or as a concession to religious convention — but as the central truth of the entire address, placed deliberately at the end as a seal, as a benediction, as a declaration of where the ultimate sovereignty resides.

Not in Geneva. Not in New York. Not in the halls of any human institution. In Jah. Always in Jah. This is the covenant. This is the closing seal of the speech. This is Haile Selassie I — King of Kings, Lord of Lords, Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah, Elect of God, Light of the World — speaking the last word to the nations of the earth, and making certain that the last word belongs to the Most High.

Jah Rastafari. Ever Living. Ever Faithful. Ever Sure. The Lion has spoken. The earth has heard. The covenant stands.

♛ Why This Speech Is Scripture The Living Covenant · The Fulfillment of Prophecy · What the Nations Witnessed

Psalm 68:31 says: Ethiopia shall soon stretch out her hands unto Jah. The Emperor of Ethiopia stretching out his hands to the assembly of all nations — speaking peace, speaking justice, speaking the covenant of Jah into the ears of every government on earth — is not a coincidence. It is fulfillment. It is the ancient word becoming present-tense reality in the body of a man who bore the titles that scripture had always reserved for the returning King of Zion.

The Rastafari tradition did not invent the connection between Haile Selassie I and the Messianic lineage. The connection was already in the text. Revelation 5:5: the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David. Revelation 19:16: King of Kings and Lord of Lords. These are not titles that were retroactively attached to Ras Tafari Makonnen by his followers. They are the titles under which he was crowned in 1930 — officially, constitutionally, in the presence of representatives from every major nation — and which he held for the rest of his life. The Rastafari movement simply read the text and looked at the man and said: this is what the text was describing. Read it yourself and see.

And then on October 4, 1963, he stood before the nations and spoke. He spoke of disarmament. He spoke of racial equality. He spoke of African unity. He spoke the "Until" passage that became the most quoted scripture in the entire reggae tradition. He closed with a benediction to Almighty God. And then he went home to Ethiopia and continued to reign. A man of whom it is written in the Ethiopian Orthodox synaxarium that even his enemies could not explain the light that surrounded him. That even those who opposed him politically could not deny the presence of something extraordinary in his bearing. That animals were calm in his presence in a way that they were not calm with other men.

The speech of October 4, 1963 is in this Bible because it belongs here. Because this is a Bible of the living covenant — not only what was written in the ancient world, but what the covenant has continued to produce in every generation of those who have kept its fire burning. Ethiopia kept the fire. Haile Selassie I carried it into the assembly of nations. Bob Marley broadcast it to the world in music. And now it is here — in this book, in this generation, going where it needs to go next.

Word Sound Power. The speech is scripture. The scripture is alive. Jah Rastafari.
