🕊 Part 1 — The Ethiopian Orthodox Daily Prayers The Seven Hours · The Ancient Liturgical Cycle · As Practised by H.1.M. Haile Selassie 1

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church has observed seven daily hours of prayer since the days of the Apostles — following the ancient pattern that David observed, that Daniel observed, and that Yeshua himself observed in the synagogue and in solitude. These are the hours of prayer as kept in the Ethiopian church and in the personal devotional life of His Imperial Majesty Haile Selassie 1. Each hour has its own character, its own orientation, its own conversation with Jah.

The Morning Prayer — At Rising O Jah my God, thou art my God. Early will 1 seek thee. My soul thirsteth for thee, my flesh longeth for thee in a dry and thirsty land where no water is. To see thy power and thy glory, so as 1 have seen thee in the sanctuary. Because thy lovingkindness is better than life, my lips shall praise thee. Thus will 1 bless thee while 1 live: 1 will lift up my hands in thy Name. My soul shall be satisfied as with marrow and fatness; and my mouth shall praise thee with joyful lips. 1 remember thee upon my bed, and meditate on thee in the night watches. Because thou hast been my help, therefore in the shadow of thy wings will 1 rejoice. My soul followeth hard after thee: thy right hand upholdeth me. Jah Rastafari. Amen.

The Third Hour Prayer — 9 AM O Holy Spirit of Jah, who descended upon the disciples at the third hour in tongues of fire — descend now upon this hour of our labour and our learning. Let every work of this day be done in thy sight and to thy glory. Let our hands be thy hands, our words thy words, our thoughts the thoughts of those who walk in awareness of the divine presence at all times and in all places. We do not ask to be removed from the world but to be made fully awake within it. Jah Rastafari. Amen.

The Sixth Hour Prayer — Noon O Jah, at this midpoint of the day — when the sun stands at its height and the light is fullest — we pause to remember that thou art the light behind all light, the sun behind the sun, the presence that the noon-day brightness only begins to suggest. Yeshua was raised on the Cross at the sixth hour. Let us remember in this moment that the highest point of the day is also the place of the most complete surrender. We lay down our agenda and take up thine. Jah Rastafari. Amen.

The Ninth Hour Prayer — 3 PM O Jah, at the ninth hour Yeshua cried from the Cross: It is finished. The veil of the temple was torn from top to bottom. What was hidden became accessible. What was reserved for the priests became available to all. At this hour we remember that access to Jah requires no intermediary — no institution, no hierarchy, no temple built by human hands. Thou art accessible to the humble heart at every hour. The veil is torn. We walk through. Jah Rastafari. Amen.

The Evening Prayer — At Sunset Jah, as the light leaves the sky and the day releases its hold upon us, we give back to thee what was never ours to keep. The events of this day — the victories and the failures, the moments of clarity and the moments of confusion — we lay them all at thy feet. We do not carry them into the night. We trust that thou art working in the darkness as in the light. Thy mercy is new every morning. We rest in that promise. Protect this household through the night. Let thy angels encamp around those who love thee. Jah Rastafari. Amen.

The Midnight Prayer Jah, at midnight Paul and Silas prayed and sang praises, and the prison doors were opened. At midnight the bridegroom came. At midnight the angel of Jah passed through Egypt. This is the hour of divine action — when Babylon sleeps and Zion prays. Rise, O soul, and pray. The darkness is not absence. It is the womb of the next day's dawn. We pray in the darkness for the light we cannot yet see. We declare before this hour is over: Jah Rastafari is King. The oppressor shall not prevail. Africa shall be redeemed. Zion shall be established. It is done in the spirit before it appears in the world. Jah Rastafari. Amen.

The Cockcrow Prayer — Before Dawn O Jah, the cock crows and we rise before the dawn as the monks of Ethiopia have risen for two thousand years — before the world stirs, before Babylon makes its demands, before the noise begins. In this holy silence before the sun appears we dedicate this day entire to thy glory. Let every breath today be a prayer. Let every meal be a sacrament. Let every meeting be a ministry. Let every word be thy word and every act an act of love. As the Ethiopian church has always said and shall always say: Holy, holy, holy — Kadus, Kadus, Kadus — is Jah the Almighty. Jah Rastafari. Amen.

🕊 Part 3 — The Prayer of Marcus Garvey Prophet of Africa's Return · The Man Who Saw Selassie Before His Coronation

Marcus Mosiah Garvey — born in Saint Ann's Bay, Jamaica, in 1887, the same parish that would thirty years later produce Robert Nesta Marley — was the great prophet of Africa's return. He founded the Universal Negro Improvement Association and African Communities League, the largest mass movement of African people in the modern era, gathering millions under the banner: Africa for the Africans — those at home and those abroad. He declared before the coronation of Haile Selassie 1: Look to Africa, for there a Black King shall be crowned and he shall be your Redeemer. The Rastafari elders who heard this prophecy recognised its fulfillment on November 2, 1930, when Ras Tafari Makonnen was crowned Emperor of Ethiopia. Garvey was the John the Baptist of the Rastafari movement — the voice crying in the wilderness, prepare ye the way of the King.

Prayer of Marcus Garvey for Africa O Jah, thou God of Ethiopia, thou God of divine majesty — thy spirit hath come down amongst us and through thy divine will, thy children from across the seas have heard thy voice and gathered together. We are assembled here before thee O God, the Jah of Ethiopia — to acknowledge our failings before thee, to confess our sins against thee and our neighbours, and to seek thy forgiveness, thy mercy and thy guidance for a new beginning in righteousness. O Jah — we return to thee, we return to thee. O Jah — let thy spirit enter our hearts so that we shall be humble, pure, and righteous before thee. O Jah — lift up the fallen, help the weak, guide the strong, and give knowledge to the wise so that all may love thee and glorify thy holy Name. Africa and Africans must be free. The hour of redemption is at hand. O Jah, hear our prayer. Lead us back to thee. Lead us back to Africa. Lead us home. Amen.

🕊 Part 4 — Nyahbinghi Invocations The Groundation Ceremony · The Opening Call · The Reasoning Circle · The Closing Blessing

The Nyahbinghi Order is the oldest and most sacred of the Rastafari orders — taking its name from a spirit of resistance and sovereignty, calling down fire on the oppressor and blessing on the righteous. The Nyahbinghi groundation ceremony is the central communal worship of the Rastafari tradition: days and nights of drumming, chanting, reasoning, and prayer around the fire, under the open sky, in the presence of Jah. These are the words spoken at the opening and closing of the groundation, and in the circle of the reasoning.

Let no discord enter this space. Let no Babylon vibration find purchase here. We light this fire in the Name of Jah. Let the fire burn. Let the drum speak. Let the word be spoken in truth. Let all who gather here gather in the Name of the Most High. Jah Rastafari. Blessed Love.

The Call of the Reasoning Circle 1 and 1 give thanks for the gift of the reasoning — this sacred space where the word is passed, where truth is tested, where the elders speak and the youth listen, where the youth speak and the elders hear, where Jah speaks through each vessel in turn and the whole is greater than the sum of its parts. Let every voice be heard. Let every truth be received. Let no ego dominate. Let no voice be silenced. The reasoning is the Sanhedrin of Zion — the council of the covenant. Let Jah preside. Speak in love. Reason in truth. Word Sound Power. Blessed Love.

The Closing Blessing of the Groundation Give thanks and praises for this holy groundation. What Jah has spoken in this circle shall not return void. What has been reasoned in truth shall take root. 🕊 Part 5 — The Prayers and Words of Haile Selassie 1 His Own Voice · His Own Prayer · The Emperor Who Knelt Before He Wore the Crown

Haile Selassie 1 was a man of prayer before he was a man of power. Every morning of his reign — through the years of triumph and the years of exile, through the coronation and the Italian invasion, through the Liberation and the final years of captivity — he knelt before the tabernacle of his private chapel and prayed before he put on the crown. These are his own words, drawn from his speeches, his autobiography, and his recorded meditations — the living prayer of a living covenant.

On the Purpose of Prayer We must go beyond the outward show of religion and make the life of prayer a reality. Prayer is not a formula of words recited by rote. It is the sincere lifting of the heart and mind to Jah. It is the acknowledgement that without Jah we are nothing and with Jah we can do all things. The man who prays in the morning and forgets Jah for the rest of the day has not yet understood prayer. The man who lives his entire day as a prayer — in every decision, every relationship, every act of service — has begun to understand what the saints have always known.

On the Centrality of Scripture The Bible is not simply a book among books or a literature among literatures. It is a living word — the breath of the living God captured in the words of those who walked with him and heard him and were transformed by him. 1 have worn the crown of the Solomonic dynasty for more than three decades. 1 have walked in palaces and 1 have walked in exile. And in every circumstance 1 have found that the scripture was not merely adequate to the hour — it was always exactly what the hour required. Open this book with that expectation and it will not disappoint.
