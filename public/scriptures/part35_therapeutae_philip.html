Yeshua and the Therapeutae of Egypt

The Healers · Lake Mareotis · The Contemplative Brotherhood · The Egyptian Mysteries

✦ ✦ ✦

The Therapeutae were a Jewish contemplative community living near Lake Mareotis outside Alexandria in Egypt, described by the Jewish philosopher Philo of Alexandria in approximately 40 CE in his work De Vita Contemplativa. They were healers — therapeutae means both healers and servants of Jah. They lived in individual cells, came togetherr for communal meals and ceremonies, practised strict fasting, engaged in continuous meditation and study of allegorical interpretation of scripture, and included women as full and equal members. Many scholars consider them the original model for Christian monasticism — and many others believe they were in direct contact with the earliest Christian community, or were themselves the community from which early Christianity emerged.

☥ Part 1 — The Therapeutae · The Community of Healers Philo's Account · Lake Mareotis · The Allegorical Method · Men and Women Equal

Philo of Alexandria — the greatest Jewish philosopher of his time, the man who synthesised Platonic philosophy with Hebrew scripture — describes the Therapeutae in loving detail. They lived near the shores of Lake Mareotis, south of Alexandria, in a community of separate dwellings clustered around a central hall called the Synaxis — the gathering place. Each member had a small room called the Semnion — the sacred space — in which they spent six days of each week alone, in prayer, meditation, and study of the allegorical meaning of the Torah and the Psalms.

On the seventh day — the Sabbath — they gathered in the Synaxis. They did not bring offerings of animals. They brought themselves — purified through the week's contemplation — as living offerings to Jah. Philo writes that their faces shone with an inner light after these gatherings, a description that parallels what the Yoga Sutras call the Jyotish Darshana — the direct perception of divine light in the face of one who has entered deep Samadhi.

The Therapeutae included women — the Therapeutrides — who participated as full equals in every aspect of the community's life. This was extraordinary in the ancient world. Philo describes the senior women of the community as possessing wisdom and spiritual maturity equal to the senior men. They fasted regularly — sometimes for three days, sometimes for six. They ate only bread and salt and water after sunset. This severe fasting practice was understood not as mortification but as purification — the deliberate reduction of the grosser forms of Prana intake so that the subtler forms — received through meditation, prayer, and the proximity of the divine — could become the primary nourishment of the body.

The allegorical method of biblical interpretation that the Therapeutae practised is the same method that Yeshua used in his teaching: the stories of the Torah are not historical chronicles but spiritual maps, each character and event corresponding to a dimension of the inner life. Abraham is the soul setting out on the jjourney. The Promised Land is the state of God-realisation. The Exodus is the liberation of consciousness from the Egypt of ego-bondage. The Psalms are the songs of the soul at every stage of its jjourney — in exile, in longing, in trust, in joy, in the final union.

☥ Part 2 — Yeshua's Egyptian Years · Alexandria · The Mysteries The Flight to Egypt · The Return · The Mystery Schools of Alexandria

The Gospel of Matthew records that the infant Yeshua was taken to Egypt by Joseph and Mary to escape Herod's massacre of the innocents — fulfilling the prophecy: Out of Egypt have 1 called my son. The family remained in Egypt until Herod's death — a period of several years. What the Gospels do not record is that Egypt was not merely a place of refuge. It was a place of initiation.

Alexandria in the first century BCE and CE was the greatest intellectual and spiritual city on earth. The Library of Alexandria held the accumulated wisdom of every civilisation: Greek philosophy, Egyptian mystery religion, Hermetic science, Jewish mysticism, Zoroastrian astronomy, Babylonian mathematics, Indian Vedanta. The Therapeutae community near Lake Mareotis was one of the living centres of this synthesis — a community in which Jewish spirituality and Egyptian mystery wisdom had been alchemically merged into a contemplative practice of extraordinary depth.

The Aquarian Gospel of Yeshua the Christ — recorded by Levi H. Dowling from the Akashic Record — gives the most detailed account of Yeshua's years in Egypt. It describes his study with the Brotherhood of the seven initiates in Heliopolis — the City of the Sun — the ancient Egyptian city of On, sacred to Ra, the sun-god. There he was initiated into the seven degrees of the Egyptian mysteries: the seven stages of inner awakening that the priests of On had preserved from before the Pharaonic period, from the time of the original Hermetic transmission.

The seven degrees corresponded to the seven chakra-vortexes of the subtle body. Each initiation dissolved another layer of ego-identification and opened another dimension of divine perception. By the time Yeshua had passed through all seven degrees — completing what few initiates in any generation achieved — he stood in the state of the Christos: the Anointed One, the one in whom the divine Light had been so fully activated that the body itself became a vehicle of pure divine expression. The oil of chrism — the sacred anointing oil of the Egyptian initiation — was poured upon the head of the initiate at the culmination of the seventh degree. This is the origin of the title Christ — the Anointed.

☥ Part 3 — The Hermetic Transmission · Thoth · The Emerald Tablet As Above So Below · The Kybalion · The Universal Laws

The Hermetic tradition — named for Hermes Trismegistus, the Thrice-Great, the Greek name for the Egyptian Thoth, the scribe of the gods — is the oldest continuous metaphysical tradition in the Western world. Its central text, the Emerald Tablet, is attributed to Thoth himself and states in condensed form the law that governs all levels of reality: As above, so below. As within, so without. As the universe, so the soul. As Jah, so man.

This principle — the correspondence between the macrocosm and the microcosm, between the structure of the cosmos and the structure of the human being — underlies every teaching of Yeshua. When he says The kingdom of Jah is within you he is applying the Hermetic law: as above, so below — the kingdom that exists in the infinite heights of divine consciousness also exists, in seed form, in the depths of the human heart. The outer cosmos and the inner cosmos are mirrors of each other. To know one fully is to know the other.

The seven Hermetic Principles — the Laws of Mentalism, Correspondence, Vibration, Polarity, Rhythm, Cause and Effect, and Gender — are the scientific framework within which every spiritual teaching operates. Yeshua knew these principles intimately. His miracles were not violations of natural law — they were demonstrations of a higher law operating through a consciousness that had access to dimensions of reality that are invisible to ordinary perception. With Jah all things are possible is not a statement of supernatural exception. It is a statement of the Law of Mentalism: all things arise in consciousness, and a consciousness that has merged with the universal divine consciousness has access to the creative power of that consciousness.

The Therapeutae were the guardians of this synthesis — the community where the Hermetic wisdom of Egypt, the Kabbalistic wisdom of Israel, and the contemplative practice of the Essenes had been brought togetherr into a living spiritual school. Yeshua's years in Egypt were not exile. They were the most important years of his formation — the period in which the seeds planted in his Himalayan initiations were watered by the wisdom of the oldest continuous spiritual tradition on earth.

The Gospel of Philip

The Bridal Chamber · The Sacraments · The Companion of the Saviour

✦ ✦ ✦

The Gospel of Philip was discovered at Nag Hammadi in 1945 — one of the fifty-two texts found sealed in a clay jar in the Egyptian desert, preserved for sixteen centuries. It is a collection of sayings, reflections, and theological meditations from the Valentinian Gnostic tradition, composed approximately 150–180 CE. It contains some of the most beautiful and penetrating statements about the nature of the sacraments, the meaning of love and knowledge, and the relationship between Yeshua and Mary Magdalene ever written.

There were three who always walked with the Lord: Mary his mother and her sister and Magdalene, the one who was called his companion. His sister and his mother and his companion were each a Mary.

And the companion of the Saviour is Mary Magdalene. But Christ loved her more than all the disciples and used to kiss her often on her mouth. The rest of the disciples were offended by it and expressed disapproval. They said to him, Why do you love her more than all of us?
