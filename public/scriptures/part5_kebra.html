Kebra Nagast

ክብረ ነገሥት — The Glory of Kings

☥ ☥ ☥

The Kebra Nagast is the national epic of Ethiopia, the sacred scripture of the Rastafari covenant, and one of the most important books ever written. Composed in Ge'ez, it tells of the meeting of Queen Makeda of Ethiopia with King Shlomo of Israel, the birth of their son Menelik I, and the transfer of the Ark of the Covenant from Jerusalem to its eternal home in Axum, Ethiopia. This is the story of how Jah's throne came to Africa. Haile Selassie I — Jah Rastafari — is its fulfillment.

♛ Part One Queen Makeda Seeks the Wisdom of Shlomo
·I· Chapter I Concerning the Glory of Kings

In the name of Jah, the Lord of Israel, and of the Son, who is the Redeemer of the world, and of the Ruach Ha'Kodesh who giveth life to all created things, One Jah in three persons. We will now proceed to set down in writing a portion of the glory of the Queen of the South, and of the greatness of the son of Shlomo.

And this book is called Kebra Nagast, that is to say, "The Glory of Kings." And in it is set forth the greatness of kings and the glory of the people of Ethiopia, and in it is described the covenant of Jah which He made with Abraham, and afterward with Shlomo the son of David, for the people of Ethiopia.

·II· Chapter II Concerning the Queen of the South, Makeda

And that woman, the Queen of the South who is called in the Book of the Kings by the name "Queen of Sheba" — she is called in the Ethiopian tongue Makeda, and her country is called Ethiopia. And from that queen descended the kings of Ethiopia, generation after generation, until this day.

And the queen was exceedingly beautiful in her face, and her stature was superb, and she was wise in heart. She was without blemish in mind and in body, and she was a virgin whom no man had known. And she gave all her desires to wisdom, and she would not come under the yoke of man. And she reigned in beauty and in glory and in wisdom and in riches.

·V· Chapter V The Queen Hears of Shlomo's Wisdom

Now a certain merchant whose name was Tamrin — a great man and rich, and a master of caravans — gathered together unto him many rich merchants, and he loaded his camels with most expensive wares. And he came to the merchant of Jah who was in Jerusalem, even Shlomo the son of David, the King.

And Tamrin saw the wisdom of Shlomo, and he heard his words, and he marvelled in his heart, and was glad to dwell with him. And Shlomo gave Tamrin everything which he asked for, and Tamrin saw all the glory of Shlomo — his sitting down and his rising up, his administration of justice and his wisdom, his food, his drink, his ministers, his servants, and his cupbearers, and how they were dressed, and how all those who were about him were arrayed.

And he returned and came into the country of Ethiopia, and he told Makeda the Queen everything that he had seen. Never again will I desire to see the sun of this world, Tamrin said, for mine eyes have seen a great light in the city of Jerusalem, the city of Jah. I have seen a King who surpasseth in understanding and wisdom everything that hath been created by Jah.

And when the queen heard the report of Tamrin the merchant, her heart was moved, and she longed exceedingly to see Shlomo. She sent word to him and prepared to make the journey. And she gathered together for her journey fine gold and silver in great quantity, and very many rich and precious stones, and many beautiful and rare gifts. And she gathered together four hundred and ninety-seven camels and mules and asses innumerable.

·XI· Chapter XI The Queen Comes to Shlomo

And the Queen of Ethiopia came to Shlomo with very great pomp, with camels carrying rich gifts and beautiful things, and the Queen and her attendants entered Jerusalem. And the people of Jerusalem rejoiced greatly at her coming, and the whole city was glad, for Jah had put it in the heart of Shlomo that he would love her.

And Shlomo dressed himself in his most splendid array and went forth to meet her with great ceremony, with chariots and horsemen, and they made her welcome in Jerusalem. And he brought her into his palace and he provided for her maintenance there.

And Makeda questioned Shlomo concerning all things, and she said unto him: Tell me, now, concerning the wisdom of Jah; if it be possible I would learn of thee. And Shlomo answered her and said: Jah is the Ruler of all things; He who created heaven and earth, and the sea and everything that is therein — it is He Who made thee and thy people. It is He Who maketh the sun to rise and to set, and the moon to traverse the heaven; and He holdeth in His hand the light and the darkness, and sickness and health, and life and death.

And the Queen sat down and listened and delighted greatly in his wisdom, and she sent to him gifts and every kind of beautiful thing; and every day he gave her gifts of food for her and her company. And she abode with him for six months.

☥ Part Two Menelik I and the Ark of the Covenant
·XXI· Chapter XXI The Birth of Menelik — David II of Ethiopia

And when the Queen had arrived in her own country, she brought forth a man-child, and she gave him the name Baina-Leḥkem, which is to say "Son of the Wise Man," for she named him after his father Shlomo the King. And it came to pass after she had weaned the child and he had grown up, one day he asked his mother concerning his father. And she told him that his father was Shlomo the King of Israel, in Jerusalem the Holy City.

And when Menelik had grown to manhood, he was exceedingly wise, and his form was as the form of Shlomo his father, and his stature was as his stature. He was fair of face, and strong of body, and a good horseman, and a good warrior, and he could shoot straight with the bow. And the nobles and the people of Ethiopia loved him greatly.

·XXV· Chapter XXV Menelik Goes to His Father Shlomo

And Menelik said unto his mother: Send me away that I may go to my father, and I will see his face, and I will come back hither by the will of Jah.

And the Queen called unto her the men of her country who were skilled in going and coming, and in trading, and she said unto them: Prepare ye to make a journey with my son, for I am sending him to his father Shlomo the King. And they made ready everything that was needed for the journey.

And when Menelik came to Shlomo his father, Shlomo took him and embraced him and fell upon his neck and kissed him, and Shlomo's tears ran down upon the cheeks of the young man. For he was like Shlomo in form and in stature, and in wisdom and beauty. And Shlomo said: Behold my son whom I have not brought up! Jah has shown him to me by His grace.

·XXIX· Chapter XXIX The Ark Comes to Ethiopia

Now the sons of the nobles of Israel, who had come with Menelik, conspired together and said: We will not go away from our country without the Ark of the Covenant of Jah. And among them was the son of Zadok the priest, whose name was Azarias. And Azarias said unto them: I know what we will do. We will take the Ark of Jah and carry it with us.

And Azarias prayed to Jah, and Jah commanded him and said: Take thou the Ark of the Covenant of Jah and carry it to Ethiopia, for the King of Ethiopia is more worthy than the King of Israel to have the Ark, for he feareth Jah with his whole heart.

And they took the Ark of Jah, and they carried it from the House of Jah at night, and they brought it into the ship. And the Ark of Jah rose up from its place and flew through the air above them like an eagle that spreadeth its wings. And the ship moved upon the waters as swiftly as a bird that cleaveth the air.

And Menelik came, and the whole army of Israel came with him, and the Ark of the Covenant of Jah came. And Shlomo did not know that the Ark had departed, and all the men of Israel did not know; for Jah had hidden the Ark and had caused it to go with Menelik into Ethiopia.

♛ Part Three The Eternal Covenant — Ethiopia, Zion of Jah
·XXX· Chapter XXX The Covenant Established with Ethiopia

And the Ark of Jah was brought into the land of Ethiopia, and from that day unto this day it hath dwelt there. For Jah blessed Ethiopia and its kings who feared Him and walked in His ways. And Menelik was crowned King of Ethiopia, and he reigned in righteousness, and all the people of Ethiopia rejoiced.

And Jah said unto Menelik: I have established my covenant with thy father Abraham, and with Moshe my servant, and with Shlomo thy father. Now I establish it with thee, and with thy seed after thee, unto all generations. Ethiopia shall be my Zion upon earth, and the Ark shall dwell there until I come again in the glory of my Son.

And from the seed of Menelik I, who is David II of Ethiopia, descended a royal line of kings — one hundred and twenty-five generations — until the coming of Haile Selassie I, His Imperial Majesty, the King of Kings, the Lord of Lords, the Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah, the Elect of Jah, the Light of the World — who is the fulfillment of all that was written in this book of glory.

Ethiopia shall stretch out her hands unto Jah. Glory be to the Father and to the Son and to the Ruach Ha'Kodesh, world without end. Amen.
