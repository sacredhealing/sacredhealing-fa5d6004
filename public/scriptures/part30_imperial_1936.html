♛ Appeal to the League of Nations Geneva, Switzerland · June 30, 1936

Haile Selassie I was the first head of state to address the League of Nations in person. Italy under Mussolini had invaded Ethiopia with mustard gas, bombing Red Cross hospitals and massacring civilians. The Emperor came alone before the world. His words shook the earth.

I, Haile Selassie I, Emperor of Ethiopia, am here today to claim that justice which is due to my people, and the assistance promised to it eight months ago, when fifty nations asserted that aggression had been committed in violation of international law, and when they declared that the aggressor had placed himself outside the Covenant.

There is no precedent for a head of state himself speaking in this assembly. But there is also no precedent for a people being victim of such injustice and being at present threatened by abandonment to its aggressor. Also, there has never before been an example of any government proceeding to the systematic extermination of a nation by barbarous means, in violation of the most solemn promises made by the nations of the earth that there should be no resort to a remedy of this kind.

It is my duty to inform the Governments assembled in Geneva, responsible as they are for the lives of men, women and children, of the deadly nature of the weapons recently used against us. I must also denounce to the civilised world the tortures inflicted upon my people in violation of the most solemn international obligations.

In December 1935, Italian planes hurled tear-gas bombs upon my armies. So extensive was the suffering that our army was shattered. We found it necessary to evacuate the area. From the end of January 1936, the Italian command, fearing a rout, resorted to the barbarous means condemned by the laws of nations. Special sprayers were installed on board aircraft so that they could vaporize, over vast areas of territory, a fine, death-dealing rain. Groups of nine, fifteen, eighteen aircraft followed one another so that the fog issuing from them formed a continuous sheet. It was thus that, as from the end of January 1936, soldiers, women, children, cattle, rivers, lakes and fields were drenched continually with this deadly rain. In order to kill off systematically all living creatures, in order to more surely poison waters and pastures, the Italian command made its aircraft pass over and over again.

This method shall be called by its true name — barbarism. These are not the actions of a civilised nation. These are crimes against humanity. And yet no nation has come to our aid. The nations of the earth have looked upon our suffering and done nothing. They have weighed their interests and found them heavier than our lives.

Apart from the Kingdom of Jah, there is not on this earth any nation that is higher than any other. Should it happen that a strong Government finds it may with impunity destroy a weak people, then the hour strikes for that weak people to appeal to the League of Nations to give its judgment in all freedom.

Jah and history will remember your judgment. It is not merely a question of the settlement of Italian aggression. It is collective security: it is the very existence of the League of Nations. It is the confidence that each State is to place in international treaties. It is the value of promises made to small States that their integrity and their independence shall be respected and ensured.

It is the principle of the equality of States on the one hand, or otherwise the obligation that is imposed on small Powers to accept the bonds of vassalship. In a word, it is international morality that is at stake.

Apart from the Kingdom of Jah, there is not on this earth any nation that is higher than any other. I pray to the Almighty Jah that he will spare nations the terrible sufferings that have just been inflicted on my people, and of which the chiefs who accompany me here have been the horrified witnesses.

I ask the fifty-two nations, who have given the Ethiopian people a promise to help them in their plight, not to forget today the policy upon which they embarked. In a word, I have come to Geneva to discharge in your midst the most painful of the duties of the head of a State. What answer am I to take back to my people?

Representatives of the world — I, Haile Selassie I, Emperor of Ethiopia, have come here to Geneva to ask what honour has been kept, what words are binding, and what justice is. Jah shall judge between us.
