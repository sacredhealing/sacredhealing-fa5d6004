Haile Selassie I — The United Nations Address

New York · October 4, 1963 · The War Speech

Haile Selassie I returned to the United Nations on October 4, 1963 — twenty-seven years after his historic 1936 League of Nations address. This speech became the foundation of Bob Marley's song War, recorded verbatim as scripture. It is one of the most powerful declarations ever delivered before the nations of the earth.

♛ The Return · October 4, 1963 · New York Twenty-Seven Years After Geneva · The League That Failed · The New Covenant

Twenty-seven years ago, as Emperor of Ethiopia, I mounted the rostrum in Geneva, Switzerland, to address the League of Nations, an organization devoted to the principle of collective security. I spoke there then of, and for, the plight of my innocent nation, invaded and despairing. I pleaded then for the chastisement of the aggressor and for the basic principles on which the Organization was founded. I asked for the support of all nations, large and small, in upholding those principles.

My plea fell on deaf ears.

The League failed. The principles of collective security were abandoned. In consequence, peace on earth was destroyed, and a world war followed, taking millions of lives and laying waste to vast regions of the earth.

Today, I stand before another organization, dedicated to the same principles, seeking to build peace and international order upon the ruins of the League. The United Nations is the modern instrument created by man to preserve peace, to champion justice, to advance human rights, and to foster human freedom.

We meet today in an atmosphere of hope, but also of grave responsibility. We must ask ourselves: Has the United Nations learned from the mistakes of the League of Nations? Are we prepared to take the steps necessary to ensure that history does not repeat itself?

The goal to which we aspire is a world at peace — a world in which disarmament is an accomplished fact, where human rights are respected by all, where equality reigns among all peoples and nations, and where economic and social justice prevail.
