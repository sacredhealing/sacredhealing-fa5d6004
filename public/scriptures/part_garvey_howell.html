THE PROPHETS OF THE RESTORATION

Marcus Mosiah Garvey · Leonard Percival Howell · The Foundation of the Rastafari Movement · The Prophecy Fulfilled

✦ Chapter 1 — Marcus Mosiah Garvey · The Prophet Before the King Born 1887 · St. Ann's Bay Jamaica · Look to Africa · The Prophecy of 1920

1:1Marcus Mosiah Garvey was born on 17 August 1887 in St. Ann's Bay, Jamaica — the same parish that would later produce Robert Nesta Marley. He came from the people. He left school at fourteen. He worked in a print shop. He read everything. And from that reading and from the suffering he saw around him, something rose in him that could not be contained — a vision of African people standing upright, governing themselves, honouring themselves, knowing themselves as the covenant people of the Most High.

1:2In 1914 in Jamaica, Garvey founded the Universal Negro Improvement Association — the UNIA. Its motto: One God, One Aim, One Destiny. By 1920 it had chapters in forty countries and over two million members. No Black organisation in history had ever moved so fast, reached so far, or spoken so clearly to what the scattered children of Israel needed to hear. Africa for the Africans — those at home and those abroad. Up, you mighty race, accomplish what you will.

1:3On 1 August 1920, at the first International Convention of the Negro Peoples of the World at Madison Square Garden in New York City, Garvey addressed 25,000 people. Delegations came from twenty-five countries. He declared the adoption of the African flag — red for the blood of the people, black for the colour of their skin, green for the richness of the land. He outlined a vision of a free Africa, a repatriated people, a restored covenant nation. And somewhere in his speeches of that period he spoke words that the Rastafari movement would later recognise as direct prophecy: Look to Africa, for there a Black King shall be crowned, and the day of deliverance is near.

1:4Ten years later, on 2 November 1930, Ras Tafari Makonnen was crowned Emperor of Ethiopia as Haile Selassie 1 — Emperor of Ethiopia, King of Kings, Lord of Lords, Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah, Elect of God, Light of the World. Every one of those titles is a title from the Book of Revelation. The man who was crowned wore the titles of the Bible on his chest. And the people who remembered Garvey's words said: Look. He told us. Look to Africa. A Black King has been crowned. The day of deliverance is near.

1:5Isaiah 46:10. Declaring the end from the beginning, and from ancient times the things that are not yet done. This is what a prophet does. Not magic. Not fortune-telling. A prophet sees the direction of the covenant, reads the scripture, reads the times, and speaks what Jah has shown them. Garvey read Psalm 68:31 — Ethiopia shall soon stretch out her hands unto Jah. He read Isaiah 11:11 — the Lord shall set his hand again the second time to recover the remnant of his people from the islands of the sea. He read the scripture. He saw what was coming. He spoke it. And it came.

1:6Garvey was convicted in 1923 on mail fraud charges widely considered politically motivated — the first Black man in America to be surveilled by J. Edgar Hoover's FBI. He was imprisoned, then deported, then marginalised. He died in London in 1940, ten years after the coronation he had prophesied, without ever seeing the movement he had planted grow into what it became. Like Moses who saw the Promised Land from the mountain but did not enter. Like John the Baptist who prepared the way but did not walk in it. The prophet does not always receive the fruit of what he plants. But the fruit comes.

✦ Chapter 2 — Leonard Percival Howell · The First Rastaman The Promised Key · 1933 · Pinnacle · The Community of Livity · What He Built

2:1Leonard Percival Howell was born in 1898 in Clarendon, Jamaica. He travelled. He served in the First World War. He went to America, to Panama, to Africa. He saw the world and came back to Jamaica carrying something — a fire that the sight of Haile Selassie 1's coronation in 1930 ignited into a blaze. Howell was the first person in Jamaica to publicly proclaim Haile Selassie 1 as the returned Messiah, the King of Kings, the fulfillment of Revelation 5:5. He did it in 1933 at a time when this proclamation could get a Black man in Jamaica arrested — and it did. He was arrested multiple times. He kept preaching.

2:2In 1935 Howell published The Promised Key — a small pamphlet, written under the name G.G. Maragh, that laid out the theological foundation of what would become Rastafari. It proclaimed Haile Selassie 1 as the living God, the Black Christ, the King of Kings prophesied in Revelation. It called for the repatriation of Africans to Ethiopia. It identified Babylon as the colonial system of oppression — spiritual, political, economic — from which the children of Israel must come out. And it rooted all of this in the King James Bible, which Howell read as a Black man's book, as the record of his own people's history, as the covenant that had been stolen and distorted and was now being restored.

2:3In 1940 Howell established the Pinnacle commune in the St. Catherine hills of Jamaica — the first Rastafari community. Hundreds of people lived there, growing food, living in Ital simplicity, worshipping Jah, reasoning together, growing their locks as the Nazirite vow of Numbers 6 instructed. The Jamaican authorities raided Pinnacle multiple times, arresting Howell and dispersing the community. Each time the community regathered. What Howell had built could not be destroyed by raiding it. The fire was already in the people.

2:4Howell understood what the elders have always known: the Bible reads differently when you read it as the oppressed. When the enslaved people of Jamaica read Exodus, they are not reading ancient history. They are reading their own story. When they read the Psalms of David — 1 will lift up mine eyes unto the hills, from whence cometh my help — they are not reading poetry. They are praying. When they read Revelation 13:10 — He that leadeth into captivity shall go into captivity — they are reading a promise that Babylon will fall. Howell gave the Bible back to the people to whom it belonged. That is what he built. That is what still stands.

2:5Revelation 18:4. And 1 heard another voice from heaven, saying: Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues. This is the Rastafari call — Come out of Babylon. Not as escapism. Not as withdrawal from life. As a conscious refusal to participate in a system built on exploitation, on the erasure of identity, on the worship of false powers. Howell saw that the children of Israel in Jamaica were living in Babylon — colonised, church-controlled, told that their African heritage was shameful and their Black God was a contradiction. He said: No. Jah is Black. The King of Kings is Ethiopian. The covenant is yours. Come out of Babylon and into yourselves.

✦ Chapter 3 — The Movement They Built · What Grew From the Seed The Three Mansions · Nyahbinghi · Bobo Ashanti · Twelve Tribes · The Living Movement

3:1From the seeds that Garvey planted in 1920 and Howell watered in 1933, the Rastafari movement grew into three great mansions — three expressions of the same covenant fire, each holding a different emphasis, each serving a different need within the community of believers.

3:2The Nyahbinghi order is the oldest mansion. It takes its name from a spiritual force — Nyahbinghi, meaning death to black and white oppressors — that the Ethiopian resistance had invoked against Italian colonialism. The Nyahbinghi hold reasoning circles — groundations — where elders and youth sit together, read the scripture, reason on its meaning, chant and drum through the night. The Nyahbinghi groundation is one of the oldest forms of democratic spiritual community in the modern world. No hierarchy. No priest class. Every Rasta speaks. Every Rasta reasons. The Most High speaks through all.

3:3The Bobo Ashanti — formally the Ethiopia Africa Black International Congress — was founded by Prince Emmanuel Charles Edwards in 1958 in the Back-O-Wall community of Kingston. Prince Emmanuel proclaimed himself the reincarnation of the priestly aspect of the Godhead — the third member of the Rastafari trinity alongside Haile Selassie 1 as King and Marcus Garvey as Prophet. The Bobo Ashanti maintain a strict community with priestly and kingly orders, Sabbath observance from Friday sunset to Saturday sunset, turbaned heads, long robes, and a commitment to complete separation from Babylonian society. They operate the only self-sufficient Rastafari commune still functioning in Jamaica — on the Blue Mountains above Kingston — maintaining what Howell began at Pinnacle.

3:4The Twelve Tribes of Israel was founded on 25 February 1968 in Trench Town, Kingston by Vernon Carrington — known to his community as Prophet Gad. Gad read the Bible from Genesis to Revelation, a chapter a day, and from that reading came the revelation of the tribal system — each person assigned to one of the twelve tribes of Jacob by birth month. The Twelve Tribes became the most internationally widespread mansion, drawing members across the Caribbean, North America, Europe, and Africa. It was through the Twelve Tribes that Bob Marley — born in February, of the tribe of Issachar — received his formal Rastafari grounding in the early 1970s. And it was through Bob Marley that the message of Rastafari reached the world.

3:5Acts 2:17. And it shall come to pass in the last days, saith Jah, 1 will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh: and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, and your young men shall see visions, and your old men shall dream dreams. This is what Garvey saw in 1920. This is what Howell proclaimed in 1933. This is what the three mansions have been holding ever since — the understanding that the Spirit of Jah is being poured out in the last days, that the restoration of Israel is happening now, that the dry bones of Ezekiel 37 are rising, that Ethiopia has already stretched out her hands. The movement they built is not a cult, not a sect, not a Jamaican cultural phenomenon. It is a covenant people waking up to who they are. It is the prophecy being fulfilled in real time, in real bodies, on real earth.
