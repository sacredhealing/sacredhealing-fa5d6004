♛ Part 4 — The Italian Invasion and the Exile 1935–1941 · Gethsemane · The Suffering Servant · The Return

In October 1935 — five years after his coronation — Benito Mussolini's Italy invaded Ethiopia without provocation or declaration of war, using mustard gas, bombing Red Cross hospitals, and massacring civilians. It was the first use of chemical weapons against a civilian population in history. The nations of the earth did nothing. The League of Nations imposed token sanctions. The great powers watched and waited.

And Haile Selassie 1 — as we have read in his League of Nations speech — stood alone before the world and called upon justice. He was refused. Ethiopia fell. He went into exile in Bath, England — a King without a kingdom, a Lion without a throne. The nations that had promised to protect him had abandoned him.

This exile is his Gethsemane. Isaiah 53 had written of the suffering servant: He is despised and rejected of men; a man of sorrows, and acquainted with grief. He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth. Haile Selassie 1 in exile bore this suffering with extraordinary dignity. He never lost his faith. He never surrendered his title. He continued to correspond with his people, to pray for Ethiopia, and to believe that Jah would restore him.

And Jah did restore him. On May 5, 1941 — exactly five years after the fall of Addis Ababa — Haile Selassie 1 re-entered his capital in triumph, riding at the head of the Ethiopian and British forces that had liberated his nation from the Italian occupation. He stood at the gates of Addis Ababa and said: On this day 1 thank Jah who has enabled me to stand in the capital of my fathers. This day is a day of rejoicing for Ethiopia. We have been separated from our land for five years. Now we have returned.

The return from exile. The restoration of the throne. This too is written — in Ezekiel 37, in the valley of dry bones where the prophet sees the dead nation restored to life: 1 will open your graves and cause you to come up out of your graves, and bring you into the land of Israel. And ye shall know that 1 am Jah. Ethiopia was the dry bones. The Italian occupation was the grave. The liberation of 1941 was the breath of Jah breathing life into the nation again.

And the Rastafari in Jamaica — who had been persecuted and imprisoned for their faith in Haile Selassie 1 during the years of exile — were vindicated. Their King had returned. Their Messiah had risen. The resurrection was not only spiritual — it was historical, visible, undeniable. Jah Rastafari had come back to his throne.

♛ Part 5 — The Reign · Pan-Africanism · The OAU Father of Africa · Builder of Nations · Voice of the Oppressed

After his restoration in 1941, Haile Selassie 1 governed Ethiopia for another thirty-three years — until 1974. These were years of extraordinary achievement. He modernised the Ethiopian state, built universities and schools across the empire, established an air force and a navy, and guided Ethiopia through the turbulent decades of African independence.

But his greatest achievement was continental. On May 25, 1963 — the same year as his United Nations speech — Haile Selassie 1 convened the founding meeting of the Organisation of African Unity in Addis Ababa. Thirty-two newly independent African nations came together under his leadership to declare the solidarity of the African continent and to commit to the liberation of all remaining colonised African peoples. He was elected the first chairman of the OAU.

May 25, 1963 is now celebrated as Africa Day across the entire continent. It is the most significant political achievement in the history of modern Africa. And it happened in Ethiopia. Under the Lion of Judah. In the city of Addis Ababa — whose name means New Flower — which Haile Selassie 1's predecessor Menelik 2 had named in hope of the flowering of a new age of African greatness.

His address at the founding of the OAU contains some of the most powerful words ever spoken on the African continent: We stand today on the stage of world affairs, before the audience of the world. Africa is no longer the dark continent, unknown and unexplored. Africa is awake. Africa is alive. Africa is no longer the property of others. Africa belongs to Africans.

He personally lobbied for the independence of Eritrea, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda, Burundi and a dozen other African nations. He welcomed liberation movements from across Africa to Ethiopia and provided them with training, funding and diplomatic support. He was the father of African independence — not in one nation but across the whole continent.

And yet he remained always the servant of Jah above all else. He built churches. He supported the Ethiopian Orthodox Church with his personal fortune. He read the Bible every day of his life. He said: The Bible is my refuge and the rallying point for all humanity. In it man will find the solution of his present difficulties and guidance for his future action. Unless he accepts with clear conscience the Bible and its great message, he cannot hope for salvation. For my part, 1 glory in the Bible.
