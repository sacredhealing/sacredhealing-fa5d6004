Tobit · Judith

ጦቢት · The Angel Raphael · The Widow Who Saved Israel

Tobit and Judith are canonical in the Ethiopian Orthodox Bible, the Catholic Bible, and the Eastern Orthodox Bible — excluded only from the Protestant canon in the sixteenth century. Tobit is one of the most beloved of all biblical stories: a righteous man struck blind, his son Tobias guided by the Archangel Raphael in disguise, a young woman Sarah delivered from a demon, and the healing of blindness through the liver of a fish. It is the story of Jah working through angels, through the natural world, and through the faithful actions of ordinary people. Judith is the story of a devout widow who used her beauty and courage to save her people from the Assyrian general Holofernes — one of the most powerful stories of feminine courage in all of scripture.

☥ Tobit · Chapters 1 — 3 Tobit's Blindness · Tobias and the Angel · Sarah's Prayer

1:1The book of the words of Tobit son of Tobiel son of Ananiel son of Aduel son of Gabael, of the descendants of Asiel and the tribe of Naphtali, who in the days of King Shalmaneser of the Assyrians was taken into captivity from Thisbe, which is to the south of Kedesh Naphtali in Upper Galilee, above Asher toward the west, and north of Phogor.

1:31, Tobit, walked in the ways of truth and righteousness all the days of my life. 1 performed many acts of charity for my kindred and my people who had gone with me in exile to Nineveh in the land of the Assyrians.

2:9That same night 1 washed myself and went into my courtyard and slept by the wall of the courtyard; and my face was uncovered because of the heat. 1 did not know that there were sparrows on the wall; their fresh droppings fell into my open eyes and produced white films. 1 went to physicians, but they did not help me.

3:2And Tobit prayed: You are righteous, O Lord, and all your deeds are just; all your ways are mercy and truth; you judge the world. And now, O Lord, remember me and look favourably upon me. Do not punish me for my sins and for my unwitting offences and those that my ancestors committed before you.

3:16At that very moment, the prayers of both of them were heard in the glorious presence of Jah. And Raphael was sent to heal both of them: Tobit, by removing the white films from his eyes so that he might see Jah's light with his eyes; and Sarah daughter of Raguel, by giving her in marriage to Tobias son of Tobit, and by setting her free from the wicked demon Asmodeus.

☥ Tobit · Chapters 4 — 12 Raphael the Healer · The Fish · The Healing of Blindness

4:5Revere Jah, my son, all your days and refuse to sin or to transgress his commandments. Live uprightly all the days of your life, and do not walk in the ways of wrongdoing; for those who act in accordance with truth will prosper in all their activities.

4:7Give alms from your possessions, and do not let your eye begrudge the gift when you make it. Do not turn your face away from anyone who is poor, and the face of Jah will not be turned away from you.

4:15And what you hate, do not do to anyone. Do not drink wine to excess or let drunkenness go with you on your way. Give some of your food to the hungry, and some of your clothing to the naked. Give all your surplus as alms, and do not let your eye begrudge your giving of alms.

5:4So Tobias went out to look for a man to go with him to Media, someone who knew the way. He went out and found the angel Raphael standing before him; but he did not know that he was an angel of Jah.

6:7The angel said to the young man, Cut open the fish and take out its gall, heart, and liver. Keep them with you and throw away the innards. For its gall, heart, and liver are useful as medicine.

11:11And Tobias smeared the gall on his father's eyes, saying, Take courage, father. And when his eyes began to smart he rubbed them, and the white films scaled off from the corners of his eyes. Then he saw his son and embraced him, and he wept and said, 1 see you, my son, the light of my eyes!

12:6Then Raphael called the two of them privately and said to them: Bless Jah and acknowledge him in the presence of all the living for the good things he has done for you. Bless and sing praise to his name. With fitting honour declare to all people the deeds of Jah. Do not be slow to acknowledge him.

12:7It is good to guard the secret of a king, but glorious to reveal and acknowledge the works of Jah. Do good and evil will not overtake you. Prayer with fasting is good, but better than both is almsgiving with righteousness.

12:151 am Raphael, one of the seven angels who stand ready and enter before the glory of Jah.

☥ Judith · Chapters 1 — 7 Holofernes Besieges Bethulia · The Crisis of Faith

4:13So Jah heard their prayers and saw their distress; the people continued fasting many days throughout Judea and in Jerusalem before the sanctuary of the Lord Almighty.

7:19Then all the people, the young men, the warriors, the women, and the children, gathered around Uzziah and the rulers of the town and cried out with a loud voice, and said before all the elders, May Jah judge between you and us! You have done us a great injury in not making peace with the Assyrians. For now we have no one to help us; Jah has sold us into their hands, to be strewn before them in thirst and exhaustion.

8:1Now in those days Judith heard about these things: she was the daughter of Merari son of Ox son of Joseph son of Oziel son of Elkiah son of Ananias son of Gideon son of Raphaim son of Ahitub son of Elijah son of Hilkiah son of Eliab son of Nathanael son of Salamiel son of Sarasadai son of Israel.

8:11Judith said to them, Listen to me, rulers of the people of Bethulia! What you have said to the people today is not right; you have even sworn and pronounced this oath between Jah and you, promising to surrender the town to our enemies unless Jah turns and helps us within so many days.

8:16Do not try to bind the purposes of Jah our Jah; for Jah is not like a human being, to be threatened, or like a mere mortal, to be won over by pleading.

☥ Judith · Chapters 8 — 16 Judith Enters the Camp · The Beheading of Holofernes · The Song of Praise

9:11For your strength does not lie in numbers, nor your might in the powerful. But you are the God of the lowly, helper of the oppressed, upholder of the weak, protector of the forsaken, saviour of those without hope.

10:3She removed the sackcloth she had been wearing, took off her widow's garments, bathed her body with water, and anointed herself with precious ointment. She combed her hair, put on a tiara, and dressed herself in the festive attire that she used to wear while her husband Manasseh was living.

12:13Holofernes said to her, Have a drink and be merry with us! Judith said, 1 will gladly drink, my lord, because today is the greatest day in my whole life.

13:7Judith went up to the bedpost near Holofernes' head, and took down his sword that hung there. She came close to his bed, took hold of the hair of his head, and said, Give me strength today, O Lord Jah of Israel! Then she struck his neck twice with all her might, and cut off his head.

13:14Then Judith cried out with a loud voice, Praise Jah, O praise him! Praise Jah, who has not withdrawn his mercy from the house of Israel, but has destroyed our enemies by my hand this very night!

16:1Then Judith began this thanksgiving before all Israel, and all the people loudly sang this song of praise. And Judith said: Begin a song to my Jah with tambourines, sing to my Lord with cymbals. Raise to him a new psalm; exalt him, and call upon his name.

16:131 will sing to my Jah a new song: O Lord, you are great and glorious, wonderful in strength, invincible. Let all your creatures serve you, for you spoke, and they were made. You sent forth your spirit, and it formed them; there is none that can resist your voice.

16:17Woe to the nations that rise up against my people! The Lord Almighty will take vengeance on them in the day of judgment; he will send fire and worms into their flesh; they will weep in pain for ever.
