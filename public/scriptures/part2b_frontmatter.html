Foreword

Why This Bible Exists · What Was Removed · What Has Been Restored

✦ The Search

I searched for the Holy Bible of Haile Selassie I. The Bible that the Emperor himself read, quoted, distributed, and built a nation upon. The sacred text of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church — the oldest Christian church on earth — preserved for two thousand years in the highland monasteries of Abyssinia, written in Ge'ez, carried in procession beneath golden umbrellas, chanted by deacons who have memorised entire books by heart.

I could not find it. Not in English. Not as a complete single volume that a seeker could hold in their hands and read from Genesis to the final transmission of the Akashic tradition. It did not exist.

What I found instead were partial translations. Academic editions. Scholarly volumes locked behind university access. Individual books published separately. The Book of Enoch here. The Kebra Nagast there. A fragment of Meqabyan somewhere else. The Rastafari Sacred Writings in pamphlets. Haile Selassie's speeches in archives. The eighteen missing years of Yeshua scattered across obscure nineteenth-century manuscripts.

The complete picture — assembled, unified, restored, presented as the living scripture it has always been — did not exist. This book is the answer to that absence. This is the Bible I was looking for. Since I could not find it, I compiled it.

✦ What Was Removed

In 1684, the Church of England removed fourteen books from the Bible and called the resulting sixty-six books complete. Those fourteen books — the Deuterocanon, the Apocrypha — included Tobit, Judith, Wisdom of Solomon, Sirach, Baruch, and the books of Maccabees. They were not removed because they were found to be false. They were removed because they were inconvenient — because they complicated the theological and political positions of the institutions that controlled the canon.

But the Ethiopian Orthodox Church — which received the Gospel directly from the Apostle Philip through the Ethiopian eunuch, before Paul reached Europe, before Rome had a church, before the Council of Nicaea had convened — never accepted that removal. Their canon of eighty-eight books had stood for fifteen hundred years before the Church of England existed. It continues to stand.

Beyond the Deuterocanon, the Ethiopian canon includes books that no Western church ever accepted but that the oldest traditions of the world recognise as scripture: the complete Books of Enoch — three of them — which are quoted in the New Testament by Jude, referenced by Peter, and which Yeshua himself knew. The Book of Jubilees. The Books of Meqabyan. The Kebra Nagast — the Glory of Kings — which records in one hundred and seventeen chapters the lineage of Solomon, the wisdom of Makeda, and the journey of the Ark of the Covenant to its resting place in Ethiopia, where it remains to this day.

These books were not lost. They were preserved. They were kept safe in Ethiopia — in Aksum, in Lalibela, in the monasteries of Lake Tana — precisely because Ethiopia was the one nation on the continent of Africa that Babylon could not fully colonise. The land was protected. And in that protected land, the full scripture was protected with it.

✦ What Has Been Restored

This edition restores the complete Ethiopian Orthodox canon of eighty-eight books — and goes further still. It adds what even the Ethiopian canon does not contain but what the living stream of sacred wisdom has always carried: the Gospel of the Holy Twelve, which preserves the teachings of Yeshua on compassion and the living food. The Gospel of Mary Magdalene. Pistis Sophia. The Essene Gospel of Peace. The Aquarian Gospel. The acts of the Apostle Thomas in India.

It adds the transmission of the Nile Valley — the 42 Declarations of Maat, the Hymn to Aten, the Poimandres of Hermes Trismegistus — which predate the Hebrew scriptures by thousands of years and from which the Hebrew scriptures themselves drew their deepest wisdom. Africa did not receive the Bible. Africa gave the world the understanding that the Bible points to.

It adds the biography and sacred words of Haile Selassie I — the man who lived this scripture, who built a nation on it, who stood before the League of Nations quoting it, who distributed it to every school in Ethiopia, who was recognised by the Rastafari movement as its fulfillment made flesh. His words are scripture. His life was scripture. Both belong in this book.

It adds the eighteen missing years of Yeshua — his journey to the East, his years of study under the Nath yogi masters of India and Nepal, his transmission from Mahavatar Babaji, the advanced pranic science that explains the miracles, the Cross, and the Resurrection as mastery of the life-force rather than as supernatural intervention from without.

It adds the Rastafari Sacred Writings — the Holy Piby, the Ital transmission, the teachings of the founding elders — because the Rastafari movement is not a modern invention. It is the re-awakening of the ancient Zion covenant in the bodies of a people who were taken from Africa in chains and who kept the fire of Jah burning through three hundred years of the worst oppression in human history. Their writings are sacred. They belong in this canon.

✦ How to Read This Bible

This is not a book to be read in one sitting. It is a book to be lived with. To be opened at random and trusted that the page you land on is the page you needed. To be read slowly, with stillness, with the willingness to let a single verse sit in the consciousness for a day before moving to the next.

If you are new to scripture — begin with Genesis, then the Psalms, then the Gospel according to Matthew. These three will give you the foundation. Then go to 1 Enoch. Then the Kebra Nagast. Then the biography of Haile Selassie I. Let each section teach you how to read the next.

If you are Rastafari — you may already know the New Testament and the Psalms. Begin with the sections you have not yet encountered: the Books of Meqabyan, the Gospel of the Holy Twelve, the Ital Living Food Transmission, and the Akashic Transmission of Haile Selassie I which closes the biography section. These were written for the depth of your livity.

If you are Ethiopian Orthodox — this edition honours your tradition above all others. The eighty-eight books of your canon are presented here with the reverence they deserve. The additions beyond your canon are offered not as replacements but as companions — the outer rivers that flow into the same ocean your church has always kept.

If you are a seeker with no tradition — welcome. You are precisely who this book was made for. Begin anywhere. Trust the frequency. Jah will guide the reading.

At the back of this edition you will find the Sacred Lexicon — a complete translation guide to every Vedic, Rastafari, Ethiopian, Gnostic and Christ Consciousness term used throughout these pages. No reader will be left behind. The wisdom of every tradition has been made accessible. The door is open. Come.

✦ A Note on the Sacred Name

Throughout this edition the sacred Name has been restored. Where the King James Bible writes LORD — a substitution inserted by translators who considered the Name too holy to speak — this edition writes Jah. This is not a theological position. It is a restoration. The Name of Jah was always in the text. The Rastafari tradition, which never accepted the substitution, has always spoken it. This edition speaks it too.

Where the name Jesus appears in other translations, this edition uses Yeshua — the original Hebrew and Aramaic name, which means Jah saves. The name Jesus is a Greek rendering of a Latin rendering of a Greek rendering of an Aramaic name. Two thousand years and three translations removed from the lips that first spoke it. Yeshua is the name his mother called him. Yeshua is the name his disciples called him. Yeshua is the name that carries within it the Name of Jah. This edition restores it.

Read these pages knowing that the Name you are reading is the Name that was always there — before the translations, before the councils, before the institutions. The living Word. The living Name. Jah.
