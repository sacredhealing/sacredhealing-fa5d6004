✦ Compiler's Preface

For many years I searched for the Bible that Haile Selassie I actually read. The Bible in which Ethiopia is not a footnote but a foundation. The Bible in which the Ark of the Covenant rests not in speculation but in documented history. The Bible in which Enoch, who walked with Jah and was taken, speaks in his own words across one hundred and eight chapters. The Bible in which Yeshua's lost years in the East are recorded by those who witnessed them.

That Bible did not exist in the English language. Not from Oxford. Not from Cambridge. Not from Zondervan. Not from any publisher, academic or commercial, in the history of English letters. The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church has preserved eighty-one books in their sacred canon — twenty-two more than the Western Protestant Bible, and fifteen more than the Roman Catholic Bible. Yet no single English volume had ever gathered them all.

This is why I compiled it.

What you hold contains the complete text of the sixty-six canonical books in the King James tradition, with one correction throughout: the Name has been restored. The divine Name rendered LORD in the English translations — itself a substitution for the original Hebrew — is here returned to Jah, the short form of the sacred Name used throughout the Psalms and carried forward in the word Hallelujah, meaning Praise Jah. And Yeshua, the Name given to the Messiah by the angel before His birth, is restored in place of the Latinised Yeshua. These are not changes. They are restorations.

Beyond the sixty-six books you will find the fourteen books of the Deuterocanon — Tobit, Judith, the Wisdom of Solomon, Sirach, Baruch, the books of the Maccabees, and the rest — present in the Ethiopian and Catholic canons, absent from the Protestant Bible since 1684. You will find the complete Book of Enoch in the R.H. Charles 1917 translation — all one hundred and eight chapters — the oldest known complete manuscript of which was found in Ethiopia. You will find the Book of Jubilees, the Life of Adam and Eve, the Conflict of Adam and Eve with Satan, the Book of Jasher, and the Ascension of Isaiah.

And you will find the Kebra Nagast — The Glory of Kings — in the complete E.A. Wallis Budge 1922 translation, all one hundred and seventeen chapters, containing the full account of the meeting between Solomon and Makeda the Queen of Sheba, the birth of Menelik I, the carrying of the Ark of the Covenant from Jerusalem to Ethiopia, and the divine covenant between Jah and the lineage of David through the Ethiopian royal house — the lineage from which Haile Selassie I, the two hundred and twenty-fifth descendant of Solomon and Makeda, was crowned King of Kings in 1930.

You will find all three books of Meqabyan — the Ethiopian Maccabees — which have never appeared in any English Bible. You will find the sacred words and life of His Imperial Majesty Haile Selassie I. You will find the Rastafari sacred writings, the Nyahbinghi invocations, and the words of the prophets of sound. You will find the account of Yeshua's years in the East — in India, in Tibet, among the Nath masters — preserved in traditions that predate the Western church. You will find the Sacred Connections showing where the New Testament writers quoted directly from 1 Enoch without attribution. And you will find a Sacred Lexicon of sixty terms at the intersection of Ethiopian, Rastafari, Vedic and Gnostic wisdom.

This is not a replacement for any tradition. It is a restoration of what was always there. The Ethiopians kept these books for two thousand years. They knew something that the councils of Nicaea and Laodicea and the Westminster Assembly chose to forget. What they kept is here, in full, in English, for the first time.

Jah Rastafari. King of Kings. Lord of Lords. Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah. Elect of Jah. Light of the World. Ever Living. Ever Faithful. Ever Sure.

— Kritagya Das · Compiled in the Year of Our Lord 2026
