1 Chronicles · 2 Chronicles

The Lineages of Israel · David and the Temple · Solomon's Glory · The Kings of Judah

The Books of Chronicles retell the history of Israel from Adam through the Babylonian exile, with particular focus on David's preparations for the Temple, Solomon's building of it, and the succession of kings in Judah. Where Samuel and Kings give the political history, Chronicles gives the spiritual and liturgical history — the Levites, the singers, the musicians, the ark, the prayers. David here is above all the organiser of divine worship — the one who prepared every detail of the house of Jah even though he was not permitted to build it himself.

1 Maccabees · 2 Maccabees

The Revolt · Judas Maccabeus · The Martyrs · The Resurrection of the Dead

The Books of Maccabees record the most dramatic period of Jewish history between the Old and New Testaments — the revolt of the Maccabean family against the Seleucid Greek king Antiochus IV Epiphanes who desecrated the Temple, outlawed the Torah, and attempted to force the Jews to abandon their faith. 1 Maccabees is the military and political history of the revolt and the establishment of the Hasmonean dynasty. 2 Maccabees is the theological and spiritual account — with the first clear Old Testament statements about the resurrection of the dead, the intercession of the saints, and prayer for the departed. Both books are canonical in the Ethiopian Orthodox Bible.

⚔ 1 Maccabees · Chapters I — III The Desecration · Mattathias Rebels · Judas Maccabeus Rises

1:41Moreover king Antiochus wrote to his whole kingdom, that all should be one people, And every one should leave his laws: so all the heathen agreed according to the commandment of the king. Yea, many also of the Israelites consented to his religion, and sacrificed unto idols, and profaned the sabbath.

1:54Now the fifteenth day of the month Casleu, in the hundred forty and fifth year, they set up the abomination of desolation upon the altar, and builded idol altars throughout the cities of Juda on every side; And burnt incense at the doors of their houses, and in the streets.

2:19Then Mattathias answered and spake with a loud voice: Though all the nations that are under the king's dominion obey him, and fall away every one from the religion of their fathers, and give consent to his commandments: Yet will I and my sons and my brethren walk in the covenant of our fathers. God forbid that we should forsake the law and the ordinances. We will not hearken to the king's words, to go from our religion, either on the right hand, or the left.

2:50Now therefore, my sons, be ye zealous for the law, and give your lives for the covenant of your fathers. Call to remembrance what acts our fathers did in their time; so shall ye receive great honour and an everlasting name.

3:3And Judas Maccabeus his son, who was called Maccabeus, had been mighty and strong from his youth up: and he was like a lion in his doings, and as a lion's whelp roaring for his prey.

3:18And Judas said: It is no hard matter for many to be shut up in the hands of a few; and with the God of heaven it is all one, to deliver with a great multitude, or a small company: For the victory of battle standeth not in the multitude of an host; but strength cometh from heaven.

3:60As shall be the will of God in heaven, so he will do. And they took the holy vessels, and the candlestick of gold, and the altar of incense, and the table, and the cup, and the bowls, and the golden censers, and the veil, and the crowns, and the golden ornament that was before the temple, and they offered great gifts.

⚔ 2 Maccabees · Chapters VI — VII The Martyrs · The Resurrection of the Dead · The Seven Brothers

6:18Eleazar, one of the principal scribes, an aged man, and of a well favoured countenance, was constrained to open his mouth, and to eat swine's flesh. But he, choosing rather to die gloriously, than to live stained with such an abomination, spit it forth, and came of his own accord to the torment.

6:28Thus departing out of this life with all his strength, manfully, for the holy and sacred laws: and Eleazar left his dying for an example of nobleness, and a memorial of virtue, not only unto young men, but unto all his nation.

7:1It came to pass also, that seven brethren with their mother were taken, and compelled by the king against the law to taste swine's flesh, and were tormented with scourges and whips. But one of them that spake first said thus: What wouldest thou ask or learn of us? we are ready to die, rather than to transgress the laws of our fathers.

7:9And when he was at the last gasp, he said: Thou like a fury takest us out of this present life, but the King of the world shall raise us up, who have died for his laws, unto everlasting life.

7:11And when he was required, he put out his tongue, and stretched forth his hands manfully. And said nobly: I had these from heaven; and for his laws I despise them; and from him I hope to receive them again.

7:14So when he was ready to die he said thus: It is good, being put to death by men, to look for hope from Jah to be raised up again by him: as for thee, thou shalt have no resurrection to life.

7:22The mother of the seven said: I know not how you were formed in my womb: for I neither gave you breath nor life, neither was it I that formed the members of every one of you. But doubtless the Creator of the world, who formed the generation of man, and found out the beginning of all things, will also of his own mercy give you breath and life again, as ye now regard not your own selves for his laws' sake.

7:28I beseech thee, my son, look upon the heaven and the earth, and all that is therein, and consider that Jah made them of things that were not; and so was mankind made likewise. Fear not this tormentor, but, being worthy of thy brethren, take thy death that I may receive thee again in mercy with thy brethren.

12:43And when he had made a gathering throughout the company to the sum of two thousand drachms of silver, he sent it to Jerusalem to offer a sin offering, doing therein very well and honestly, in that he was mindful of the resurrection.

12:44For if he had not hoped that they that were slain should have risen again, it had been superfluous and vain to pray for the dead.
