Mary Magdalene is the most misrepresented figure in the entire history of Christianity. She was not a prostitute — that slander was invented by Pope Gregory I in 591 CE, six hundred years after her death, in a sermon that confused her with two other women in the Gospels. She was the most spiritually advanced of all the disciples. She was the first witness to the Resurrection. She was the one to whom Yeshua revealed the deepest teachings — teachings so advanced that even Peter could not receive them. She was, in the language of the Gnostic Gospels, the one whom Yeshua loved more than all the rest, the one he kissed on the mouth, the one he called the Woman of Light. The suppression of Mary Magdalene was the suppression of the Sacred Feminine — the removal of the divine mother principle from Christianity — and it has cost humanity two thousand years of spiritual imbalance.

✦ Part I — Who She Was Mary of Magdala · The Healed · The Devoted · The First Witness

Mary came from Magdala — a prosperous fishing town on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee, known in Aramaic as Migdal, meaning tower. She was a woman of means — the Gospel of Luke records that she was among the women who supported Yeshua and his disciples from their own resources. She was educated, independent, and spiritually gifted in ways that the male disciples were not.

The Gospels record that Yeshua cast seven demons from her. In the language of the first century, seven demons does not mean possession by malevolent spirits — it means the dissolution of the seven veils of ego-consciousness, the seven layers of separative identity that prevent the soul from perceiving its unity with Jah. Yeshua did not exorcise a sick woman. He initiated a highly prepared soul into the first level of liberation. The seven demons cast out were the seven chakric blockages dissolved — and what remained was a consciousness of extraordinary clarity and light.

From that moment she followed Yeshua everywhere. She was present at the Crucifixion when most of the male disciples had fled. She was present at the Burial. She was the first to arrive at the empty tomb on the morning of the Resurrection. She was the first to whom Yeshua appeared after his rising. She was the one he commissioned to tell the others — Go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father, and your Father; and to my God, and your God. The earliest Christian tradition called her Apostola Apostolorum — Apostle of the Apostles — the one sent to send the others.

She was not his housekeeper or his follower in the ordinary sense. She was his closest disciple, his spiritual consort in the deepest non-physical sense, and — as the Gospel of Philip reveals — the one to whom he transmitted the inner teachings that the outer disciples were not yet prepared to receive. In the Gnostic tradition she is the Sophia-principle incarnate — the divine wisdom herself, walking beside the Logos, completing the sacred dyad of masculine and feminine divine expression.

✦ Part II — The Gospel of Mary The Berlin Codex · c. 120 CE · The Vision · The Silence of Peter

The Gospel of Mary was discovered in Egypt in 1896 — the Berlin Codex — written in Coptic, translated from a Greek original composed approximately 120 CE. Large portions are missing: pages 1–6 and 11–14 are lost. What remains is among the most important spiritual texts ever recovered. It gives a picture of Mary Magdalene as a teacher of the inner path — superior in spiritual understanding to all the male disciples — and records the private teaching Yeshua gave her in a vision after the Resurrection.

The Gospel of Mary, Chapter 4: Peter said to Mary, Sister, we know that the Saviour loved you more than the rest of women. Tell us the words of the Saviour which you remember — which you know but we do not, nor have we heard them. Mary answered and said, What is hidden from you I will proclaim to you. And she began to speak to them these words: I, she said, I saw the Lord in a vision and I said to him, Lord, I saw you today in a vision. He answered and said to me, Blessed are you, for you did not waver at the sight of me. For where the mind is, there is the treasure. I said to him, Lord, does one who sees the vision see it with the soul or with the spirit? The Saviour answered and said, One does not see with the soul nor with the spirit, but with the mind which is between the two — and it is what sees the vision.

The teaching that follows — of the soul's ascent through the four powers of Darkness, Desire, Ignorance, and the Wrath of Death — is the precise map of what the Kriya sciences call the dissolution of the four lower sheaths of consciousness as the awakened Kundalini rises to the crown. Mary received this teaching because she was the only disciple sufficiently advanced to ask the right questions and to hold the right inner space for the answers.

After Mary finishes speaking, Andrew says he does not believe these words. Peter says: Did he really speak with a woman without our knowledge and not openly? Are we to turn about and all listen to her? Did he prefer her to us? And Levi answers: Peter, you have always been hot-tempered. Now I see you contending against the woman like the adversaries. But if the Saviour made her worthy, who are you to reject her? Surely the Saviour knows her very well. That is why he loved her more than us. This exchange is the history of Christianity in miniature: the male hierarchy suppressing the feminine wisdom, and the feminine wisdom being vindicated by those who actually understood what Yeshua was teaching.

✦ Part III — The Sacred Feminine in the Scripture Sophia · Shekinah · The Holy Spirit as Mother · The Divine Dyad

The Sacred Feminine — suppressed in Western religion but preserved in the mystical traditions of every culture — is not a modern invention or a feminist revision. It is the oldest theology on earth. In the Hebrew tradition, the Shekinah — the divine presence — is grammatically and spiritually feminine. She is the feminine face of Jah that dwells within the created world, within the Temple, within the human heart. When the Shekinah departs, the light goes out. When she returns, the divine presence is restored.

In the Gnostic tradition, Sophia — the divine Wisdom — is the feminine emanation of the infinite who descends into matter and must be redeemed. The story of Sophia's fall and redemption is the story of the soul's exile and return — and it is the story that Yeshua came to enact. He is the Logos — the divine Word — coming to find and restore the Sophia — the divine Wisdom — who has been lost in the darkness of matter. Mary Magdalene, in this tradition, is the earthly embodiment of Sophia — the Wisdom-principle herself, incarnate as a woman of Galilee, recognised and restored by the Logos made flesh.

In the Aramaic language that Yeshua spoke, the word for Spirit — Ruach — is feminine. The Holy Spirit is grammatically, spiritually, and energetically feminine. The original Gospel of the Hebrews, quoted by early church father Origen, records Yeshua saying: My mother, the Holy Spirit, took me by one of my hairs and carried me to the great mountain of Tabor. The Holy Spirit as Mother — not as an abstract force but as the divine feminine principle, the Shakti of the Vedic tradition, the Ruach ha-Kodesh of the Hebrew tradition — is the missing half of Christian theology. Mary Magdalene kept this teaching alive. The suppression of Mary was the suppression of the Mother.

The Song of Solomon — canonical in both the Hebrew and Ethiopian Orthodox Bible — is the scripture of the Sacred Feminine. I am black and I am beautiful, O daughters of Jerusalem. My beloved is mine and I am his. Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth, for thy love is better than wine. The Beloved in the Song is the Soul — the Sophia — longing for union with the divine. The King who comes to her chamber is Jah himself. Their union — the hieros gamos, the sacred marriage — is the highest state of mystical consciousness: the complete union of the individual soul with the universal soul, the merger of the Shakti with Shiva, of Mary with the Christ, of the human with the divine. This is what Yeshua came to restore. This is what Mary Magdalene embodied. This is what two thousand years of institutional religion has been attempting to suppress — and failing, because the truth of the Sacred Feminine cannot be suppressed. It rises, as all true things rise, in its own time.
