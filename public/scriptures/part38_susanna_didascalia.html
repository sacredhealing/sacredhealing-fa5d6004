Susanna · The Prayer of the Righteous · Bel and the Dragon

The Additions to Daniel — Preserved in the Ethiopian Orthodox Canon

These three books are additions to the Book of Daniel, preserved in the Septuagint and in the Ethiopian Orthodox canon but removed from the Protestant Bible in 1684. Susanna is the story of a righteous woman falsely accused — and of a young Daniel who rises to defend her truth. The Prayer of Azariah and the Song of the Three Holy Children is the prayer spoken inside Nebuchadnezzar's furnace. Bel and the Dragon reveals Daniel's wisdom in exposing false gods. Together they complete the testimony of Daniel as the Ethiopian Church has always known it.

✦ Susanna · The Complete Story The Righteous Woman · The False Elders · Daniel Rises to Defend Her

1There dwelt a man in Babylon called Joacim: And he took a wife, whose name was Susanna, the daughter of Chelcias, a very fair woman, and one that feared Jah.

3Her parents also were righteous, and taught their daughter according to the law of Moses.

5The same year were appointed two of the ancients of the people to be judges. Of these came the saying of the Lord: Wickedness came from Babylon from ancient judges, who seemed to govern the people.

7These kept much at Joacim's house: and all that had any suits in law came unto them. Now when the people departed away at noon, Susanna went into her husband's garden to walk.

11Then the two elders saw her going in every day, and walking; so that their lust was inflamed toward her. And they perverted their own mind, and turned away their eyes, that they might not look unto heaven, nor remember just judgments.

19Now when the maids were gone forth, the two elders rose up, and ran unto her, saying: Behold, the garden doors are shut, that no man can see us, and we are in love with thee; therefore consent unto us, and lie with us. If thou wilt not, we will bear witness against thee, that a young man was with thee: and therefore thou didst send away thy maids from thee.

22Then Susanna sighed, and said: 1 am straitened on every side: for if 1 do this thing, it is death unto me: and if 1 do it not, 1 cannot escape your hands. It is better for me to fall into your hands, and not do it, than to sin in the sight of Jah.

44And Jah heard her voice. Therefore when she was led to be put to death, Jah raised up the holy spirit of a young youth, whose name was Daniel: And he cried with a loud voice: 1 am clear from the blood of this woman.

48Then Daniel said unto them: Put these two aside one from another, and 1 will examine them. So when they were put asunder one from another, he called one of them, and said unto him: O thou that art waxen old in wickedness, now thy sins which thou hast committed aforetime are come to light.

60And they arose against the two elders, for Daniel had convicted them of false witness by their own mouth: And according to the law of Moses they did unto them in such sort as they maliciously intended to do to their neighbour: and they put them to death. Thus the innocent blood was saved the same day.

63Therefore Chelcias and his wife praised Jah for their daughter Susanna, with Joacim her husband, and all the kindred, because there was no dishonesty found in her. From that day forth was Daniel had in great reputation in the sight of the people.

✦ Bel and the Dragon Daniel Exposes the False Priests · The Living God · The Den of Lions

1And king Astyages was gathered to his fathers, and Cyrus of Persia received his kingdom. And Daniel conversed with the king, and was honoured above all his friends.

3Now the Babylonians had an idol, called Bel, and there were spent upon him every day twelve great measures of fine flour, and forty sheep, and six vessels of wine. And the king worshipped it and went daily to adore it: but Daniel worshipped his own God.

5And the king said unto him: Why dost not thou worship Bel? Who answered and said: Because 1 may not worship idols made with hands, but the living God, who hath created the heaven and the earth, and hath sovereignty over all flesh.

21Then was Daniel taken, and cast into the lions' den: and he was there six days. And in the den there were seven lions, and they had given them every day two carcases and two sheep: which then were not given to them, to the intent they might devour Daniel.

34Then the king was exceeding glad for him, and commanded that Daniel should be taken up out of the den. So Daniel was taken up out of the den, and there was no manner of hurt found upon him, because he believed in his God.

41Then said the king: Great art thou, O Jah thou God of Daniel, and there is none other besides thee. And they were all destroyed that day.

The Ethiopic Didascalia

The Teaching of the Apostles — The Order of the Living Church

The Ethiopic Didascalia — the Teaching of the Apostles — is one of the foundational church-order texts of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, preserved in Ge'ez and considered part of the broader canonical tradition. It records the teachings and ordinances transmitted by the Apostles for the governance, worship, prayer, fasting, and community life of the early church. It sits alongside the Sinodos and the Books of the Covenant as the living constitution of the Ethiopian church — the practical expression of how the covenant is lived day to day, generation to generation.

✦ The Didascalia · Part 1 — The Teaching on Prayer and Fasting The Apostolic Order · Prayer Without Ceasing · The Fast as Sacred Technology

These are the ordinances and the teaching which the holy Apostles set down for the churches: that all who believe and are baptised in the Name of Jah the Father and of Yeshua the Son and of the Holy Spirit of Truth shall observe these things without wavering and without compromise, for they are the living order of the covenant transmitted from the mouth of the Lord through those who walked with him and ate with him and received from him the full transmission of the Way.

On Prayer: Let every believer pray seven times in the day — at rising, at the lighting of the lamp in the morning, at the third hour, at the sixth hour, at the ninth hour, at evening, and at midnight. For these are the hours when the ancient ones prayed and the Lord himself observed the hours of prayer, and the prayers of the faithful at these hours ascend as incense before the throne. Let no hour be neglected, for the unceasing prayer is the breath of the soul — the Prana of the inner life maintained between the human vessel and Jah.

On Fasting: The fast is not an emptying but a filling — a deliberate clearing of the lower appetites so that the higher faculties may receive what the ordinary state of satiation obscures. When the body is lightened of its burden of digestion, the Pranamaya Kosha — the energy body — becomes clear and receptive. The voice of Jah that was present all along but obscured by the static of consumption becomes audible. Fast with joy. Fast with intention. Fast as the prophets fasted — not as those who seek to be seen fasting, but as those who seek to see Jah.

On the Community: Let the believers not forsake the gathering of the assembly, for the community is the living body of the covenant on earth. When two or three are gathered in the Name, the Shekinah descends. The gathering is not a formality — it is a technology. The combined field of sincere worshippers creates a vibrational container within which the divine presence can manifest in a way that the individual in solitude cannot generate alone. The Nyahbinghi groundation, the Ethiopian Timkat, the Sabbath gathering — all are expressions of this ancient apostolic understanding that the community is the living temple.

✦ The Didascalia · Part 2 — The Teaching on Bishops, the Poor and the Widow The Servant-Leader · The Sacred Obligation to the Poor · The Widow as Sacred Trust

Let the bishop be blameless in all things — not given to wine, not quarrelsome, not a lover of money, but gentle and peaceable, a teacher, patient with all, turning away from evil. For the bishop is the servant of the servant of Jah, not the lord of the people but their minister — one who stands in the gap between the community and the divine, whose authority comes entirely from the quality of his service and not at all from the title conferred upon him. The title without the service is Babylon in sacred clothing.

Let the care of the poor never depart from the assembly. The tithe of the community belongs first to those who have nothing — to the widow, to the orphan, to the stranger, to the sick. For Jah is the defender of the poor and the father of the orphan, and any church that accumulates wealth while its members lack bread has already departed from the apostolic order, whatever the beauty of its liturgy or the height of its steeple. The measure of a true community is not its doctrine but its care for the least among it.

Let the widow be honoured as the altar of Jah — for her prayer is pure, unencumbered by the negotiations of worldly ambition, rising clean and direct to the throne. When the assembly honours its widows, the prayers of the widows cover the assembly like a shield. When the widows are neglected, the prayers of the assembly are hindered. This is the apostolic understanding: the integrity of the community's relationship with its most vulnerable members is the foundation of its relationship with Jah.
