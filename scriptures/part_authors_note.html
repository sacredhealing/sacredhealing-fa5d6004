A Note from the Heart

Kritagya Das · 2026

1 was searching for the real Bible. Not the one that was edited by kings and councils and committees with agendas. The real one. The full one. The one that answered the questions that the 66-book version left hanging in the air without answer.

1 know what was done. 1 know that the agenda — and there was an agenda — took away important scriptures from the Bible the same way they took away eighteen years from the life of Yeshua. The silent years. The missing books. The removed texts. The hidden lineage. All of it pointing in the same direction: Africa. Ethiopia. The truth that was always there, preserved in highland monasteries, chanted in Ge'ez, carried in procession under golden umbrellas — while the West was building an empire on an incomplete text.

1 wanted to bring it back. Not as an academic project. Not as a religious statement. As a living scripture — the kind you hold and feel the frequency move through you. The kind that makes you stop reading and sit in silence because something landed so deep in the Anandamaya Kosha that the mind needs a moment to catch up to what the soul already received.

1 am also interested only in the real truth. Not tradition for the sake of tradition. Not religion for the sake of belonging. Truth. The kind that cuts through every label you were given and reaches the part of you that was there before the label — the part that Jah made, not Babylon. 1 want to connect with the real tribes. The real people who can hold real truth without needing it filtered through an institution first.

And at the end of this book — past all 88 canonical books, past the Enochic trilogy, past the Kebra Nagast, past the Rastafari sacred writings, past the saints and the transmissions — you will find deeper truths and deeper secrets. Not to replace what the scripture says. To open it. To show that what Ethiopia preserved and what India preserved and what the desert fathers discovered and what the mystics of every tradition knew — it is one truth. One fire. Many lamps.

So no matter what religion you belong to, no matter what tradition, no matter what name you were given for Jah — you can find yourself in these pages. Because the truth that this scripture carries does not belong to one people or one church or one century. It belongs to every soul that has ever looked up at the sky and felt the presence of something so vast and so loving that no name was adequate. This book is for that soul.

With my sincere heart — Kritagya Das · 2026
