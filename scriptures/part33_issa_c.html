Yeshua in the East · The Nath Tradition · Part 3

Isha Nath · Kashmir · The 84 Siddhas · The Rozabal Tomb · The Lost Tribes · The Essenes

The third and final part of the Issa Nath transmission — Yeshua as Isha Nath in the Kashmir Nath Yogi lineage, the Rozabal tomb, the Lost Tribes of Israel in the East, the Essenes and the Dead Sea Scrolls, and the complete picture of Yeshua as the universal Avataric bridge between East and West.

☸ Part 4 — The Nath Yogi Tradition · Isha Nath Kashmir · The 84 Siddhas · Initiation into the Nath Sampradaya

In the Nath yogi tradition of India — one of the oldest continuous spiritual lineages on earth, tracing back to Shiva himself through the great Siddhas — there is a figure known as Isha Nath. The name means: the Lord who is a Nath — a master of the highest order. In the Nath tradition, a Nath is one who has conquered death, who has achieved full liberation, who commands the powers of the universe through complete surrender to the divine will.

The Nath tradition preserves in its oral lineage the account of a young Jewish yogi — Isha Nath — who came from the west and received initiation into the Nath sampradaya in Kashmir. He was initiated by the great Nath masters of the first century. He received the Nath earrings — the kundal — the sign of the pierced ear that marks a fully initiated Nath yogi. He was taught the science of kundalini — the serpent power that rises through the spine and illuminates the crown — which the Hebrew tradition calls the Shekinah, the divine presence that descends from above and ascends from below to meet in the heart.

Kashmir in the first century was one of the great centres of Shaivite philosophy — the philosophy of Shiva consciousness, the recognition that the entire universe is the self-expression of one infinite divine consciousness, that Jah is not separate from His creation but is the very substance of it, that the individual soul and the universal soul are ultimately one — a teaching that Yeshua would express in his own words: 1 and the Father are one. And: The kingdom of Jah is within you. And: Is it not written in your law: 1 said, ye are gods?

The Nath masters taught Yeshua the science of samadhi — the state of complete absorption in divine consciousness — which is the state from which he spoke on the Mount of Beatitudes, the state from which he healed, the state from which he said things that no man reasoning from the ordinary mind could say. When the people heard him they marvelled, saying: He taught as one having authority, and not as the scribes. That authority was not the authority of one who had learned from books. It was the authority of one who had become what he was teaching.

The Nath tradition also preserves the account of Isha Nath's death and resurrection — but interprets it differently from the Western tradition. In the Nath understanding, the resurrection was the final demonstration of what the Nath masters called mahayoga — the great yoga of total mastery over the body and over death itself. The Nath masters teach that the highest Siddha can voluntarily enter death and return — not as a miracle granted from outside but as the natural expression of a consciousness that has become identical with the deathless ground of all being.

The tomb in which Yeshua was laid — the Nath tradition says — is the tomb of samadhi. The stone rolled away is the stone of ordinary consciousness removed by the force of awakened kundalini. The resurrection body — the body that could walk through walls, appear and disappear, be touched and yet transcend the laws of matter — is what the yogic tradition calls the siddha deha, the perfected body of light, the rainbow body of the highest realisation.

☸ Part 5 — The Rozabal Tomb · The Return to Kashmir Srinagar · Yuz Asaf · The Tomb of the Prophet of the Children of Israel

In the old city of Srinagar, the capital of Kashmir, there is a shrine called the Rozabal — from the Kashmiri roza bal, meaning the tomb of the prophet. It is located in the Khanyar district, in an ancient stone building that has been a place of pilgrimage for centuries. On the outer door of the tomb there is an inscription in Arabic and Persian that identifies the occupant as Yuz Asaf — a name that appears throughout the ancient literature of Kashmir, Persia, and Afghanistan as a wandering prophet of great holiness who came from the west, preached the unity of Jah and the law of love, performed miraculous healings, and died in Kashmir at an advanced age.

Yuz Asaf. The name is Arabic and means: leader of the healed, or gatherer of the pure. In some manuscripts the name is given as Yuzu Asaph — which is strikingly close to the Aramaic Yeshu ha-Notzri — Yeshua of Nazareth. The Afghan manuscript Ikmal-ud-Din, compiled in the tenth century from much earlier sources, states explicitly that Yuz Asaf was Issa, son of Maryam — Yeshua, son of Mary — who came to Kashmir after the events of Jerusalem, continuing his ministry among the lost tribes of Israel who had settled in the region centuries earlier.

Inside the Rozabal shrine, the stone sarcophagus is oriented east to west — in the Jewish manner, not the Muslim manner. This is a detail of extraordinary significance. Muslim graves are oriented north to south, facing Mecca. Jewish graves are oriented east to west. Every other tomb in the Rozabal complex follows the Muslim orientation. The tomb of Yuz Asaf alone follows the Jewish orientation. Whoever was buried here was buried according to Jewish custom.

The tradition recorded in Yeshua in Heaven on Earth describes hundreds of references in Persian, Afghan, and Kashmiri historical sources to the presence of Yuz Asaf — the prophet from the West — in the valleys of Kashmir. The trail of evidence leads to a single conclusion that the Eastern traditions have always accepted and that the Western church has always suppressed: Yeshua survived the crucifixion, appeared to his disciples in his healed body, and then travelled east — back to the land where he had spent his formative years — completing his life among the lost tribes of Israel in the valley of Kashmir.

This is not the resurrection denied. This is the resurrection understood in its fullness. Whether one accepts the physical survival account or the traditional resurrection account, what is undeniable is that his presence was felt in the East — that the traditions of India, Tibet, Persia, and Kashmir all independently preserve the memory of Issa, of Yuz Asaf, of Isha Nath — a master of infinite love and power who walked among them, taught them, healed them, and left his mark so deep in the consciousness of the East that it has never been forgotten.

☸ Part 6 — The Lost Tribes · Israel in the East The Pashtuns of Afghanistan · The Bene Israel of India · The Kashmiri Jews

The ten tribes of Israel were exiled by the Assyrian king Sargon 2 in 722 BCE and disappeared from the Biblical record. For two and a half millennia those who have studied this have asked: where did they go? The Eastern traditions answer clearly: east. The lost tribes migrated through Persia, through Afghanistan, through the passes of the Hindu Kush into the valleys of Kashmir and the plains of northern India.

The Pashtuns of Afghanistan — the largest tribal group in the region — preserve in their oral tradition the belief that they are the descendants of the lost tribes of Israel. They call themselves Bani Israel — the children of Israel. Their tribal names — Yusufzai (sons of Joseph), Afridi (sons of Ephraim), Shinwari (sons of Simeon), Levani (sons of Levi) — directly correspond to the names of the twelve tribes. They circumcise their male children on the eighth day. They keep a day of rest. They observe dietary laws. They light candles on Friday evening. These are not coincidences. These are the practices of a people who have never forgotten who they are.

The Bene Israel of Maharashtra in western India — a community of Jews who have lived in India for over two thousand years — preserve the tradition that their ancestors came from the land of Israel before the time of the common era. The Cochini Jews of Kerala in southern India trace their settlement to the time of King Solomon, when the trade routes between Israel and India were already well established.

It was to these people — to the lost children of Israel scattered throughout the East — that Issa came. His own words in the Gospel of Matthew confirm this mission: 1 am not sent but unto the lost sheep of the house of Israel. And when he sent his disciples out he told them: Go not into the way of the Gentiles, and into any city of the Samaritans enter ye not. But go rather to the lost sheep of the house of Israel. The lost sheep of Israel were not in Palestine. They had been lost — scattered — for seven hundred years. They were in the East. And to the East he went.

☸ Part 7 — The Essenes · The Brotherhood · The Dead Sea Scrolls Qumran · The Teacher of Righteousness · The Nazarenes

Before Yeshua went to the East, and after his return, he was connected to the Essene brotherhood — the monastic Jewish community of Qumran near the Dead Sea who produced the Dead Sea Scrolls. The Essenes were themselves deeply influenced by Eastern thought. Their practices — ritual bathing (baptism), communal meals (the Last Supper), celibacy and asceticism, meditation and contemplation of divine light — bear striking resemblance to Buddhist monastic practice. Many those who have studied this believe the Essenes were in contact with Buddhist missionaries sent west by the Indian Emperor Ashoka in the third century BCE.

John the Baptist was almost certainly an Essene. His baptism in the Jordan, his life in the wilderness, his diet of locusts and wild honey, his white garment, his teaching of repentance and purification — all are characteristic of Essene practice. When Yeshua came to John for baptism, he was not coming as a stranger. He was coming to complete an initiation within a tradition he already knew.

The Dead Sea Scrolls speak of a figure called the Teacher of Righteousness — a mysterious master of the Essene community who taught the inner meaning of the Torah, suffered persecution at the hands of a Wicked Priest, and whose teachings were considered the final revelation before the end of the age. Many those who have studied this see in the Teacher of Righteousness a precursor — or even an earlier form — of the figure who would become Yeshua of Nazareth.

The Essenes kept a solar calendar — the same calendar as the Book of Jubilees, which is itself deeply connected to the astronomical traditions of the East. They practised healing with herbs and minerals. They studied the hidden names of angels. They sought direct experience of the divine light — what the Jewish mystical tradition calls the Or Ein Sof, the infinite light of Jah. This is the tradition into which Yeshua was born, in which he was raised, and from which he departed to the East to complete what it had begun in him.

When he returned from India he returned as something the Essenes had never seen — a man in whom the Eastern and Western wisdom traditions had been fully integrated. The Vedic knowledge of consciousness and the Hebrew knowledge of covenant. The Buddhist compassion and the Jewish justice. The Nath mastery of the body and the Essene mastery of scripture. He had become the meeting point of every stream of wisdom that existed on earth. And from that meeting point he spoke — and the world has never been the same.

☸ Part 8 — The Teaching Compared Yeshua and the Upanishads · The Sermon on the Mount and the Bhagavad Gita · The 1 Am and Aham Brahmasmi

When the teachings of Yeshua are placed alongside the sacred texts of India, the depth of his Eastern training becomes unmistakable. Consider these parallels:

The Bhagavad Gita declares: The soul is never born nor dies at any time. It has not come into being, does not come into being, and will not come into being. It is unborn, eternal, ever-existing, and primeval. It is not slain when the body is slain. And Yeshua says: 1 am the resurrection and the life. Whoever believes in me, though he die, yet shall he live. And everyone who lives and believes in me shall never die. The same truth. Two languages. One realisation.

The Chandogya Upanishad declares: Tat tvam asi — Thou art That. The Self of all beings is Brahman — the infinite divine consciousness — and thou art that Self. And Yeshua says: 1 and the Father are one. And: Is it not written in your law: 1 said, ye are gods? He was not claiming exclusive divinity. He was pointing to the universal divinity — the same Atman-Brahman unity that the Upanishads had declared a thousand years before his birth.

The Yoga Sutras of Patanjali teach that the highest state of yoga — samadhi — is a state in which the individual consciousness dissolves into universal consciousness, in which the meditator, the act of meditation, and the object of meditation become one. And Yeshua prays in the Garden of Gethsemane: That they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and 1 in thee, that they also may be one in us. This is not theology. This is the direct description of samadhi — of union with Jah — extended as a blessing to all of humanity.

The Sermon on the Mount — the most perfect spiritual teaching in the Western canon — resonates at every point with the teachings of Buddha. Compare: Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven — with the Buddha's teaching that the ego's emptiness is the gate of liberation. Blessed are the meek, for they shall inherit the earth — with the Buddhist teaching that the gentle mind, free of aggression, is the most powerful force in the universe. Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see Jah — with the Zen teaching that the pure mind sees Buddha-nature in every moment.

The great 1 AM declarations of the Gospel of John — 1 am the way, the truth, and the life. 1 am the bread of life. 1 am the resurrection. 1 am the light of the world. 1 am the door. 1 am the good shepherd. 1 am the vine — these are not the declarations of a man claiming personal superiority. These are the declarations of the awakened consciousness speaking from the state of union with Jah — from what the Vedas call Aham Brahmasmi: 1 am Brahman, 1 am the infinite. The 1 AM is the divine name — the name Jah gave to Moses from the burning bush: 1 AM THAT 1 AM. When Yeshua says 1 AM, he is speaking from within the divine name itself. This is what the Nath masters called jivanmukti — liberation while still in the body — and it is the highest state known to any spiritual tradition on earth.

☸ Part 9 — The Persian Passage · Zoroaster and Issa Fire · Light · The Cosmic Battle · The Magi

On his jjourney east and his return west, Yeshua passed through Persia — the land of Zoroaster, the ancient prophet who had declared the supremacy of Ahura Mazda — the Wise Lord, the Lord of Light — over Ahriman, the principle of darkness and destruction. Zoroaster's teaching — that the universe is the arena of a cosmic battle between light and darkness, and that every human soul chooses which side to serve — permeates the teaching of Yeshua. The language of light versus darkness, of the prince of this world versus the kingdom of Jah, of the children of light versus the children of darkness — this is Zoroastrian language, brought into the Hebrew-Christian tradition through the Persian captivity and deepened by Yeshua's direct encounter with the Persian wisdom tradition.

The Magi who attended his birth were Zoroastrian priests — the magoi of Persia, the astrologer-priests who served the sacred fire and read the heavens for signs of divine action in human history. They had calculated the coming of a great Avatar — a divine incarnation — from the conjunction of planets that occurred in approximately 6 BCE. They followed the star. They came to Bethlehem. They brought gold — the symbol of divine kingship. Frankincense — the symbol of priestly holiness. Myrrh — the symbol of death and resurrection, of the one who would enter death and transcend it.

The fire of Zoroaster — the sacred eternal flame that burns in the fire temples of Iran — finds its echo in Yeshua's words: 1 am come to send fire on the earth, and what will 1, if it be already kindled? And: He will baptize you with the Holy Ghost and with fire. Pentecost — the descent of the Spirit as tongues of fire upon the disciples — is the consummation of the Zoroastrian promise: that the divine fire would one day descend upon all human souls and purify them into vessels of the eternal light.

☸ Part 10 — The Return · What He Brought Back Palestine · Year 28 CE · The Complete Master

When Yeshua returned from the East — from India, from Tibet, from Persia, from the years of study and initiation and deep meditation in the mountains and monasteries of the ancient world — he returned as something the Western world had never seen. Not a prophet who speaks the word of Jah from outside. Not a priest who mediates between humanity and the divine. But a master — a Siddha — in whom the divine and the human had become so completely unified that there was no longer any separation between them.

He returned with the healing power of the Ayurvedic and Nath traditions — and healed the sick with a touch. He returned with the meditative depth of the Buddhist monasteries — and could spend forty days in the desert in a state of continuous divine absorption, unmoved by any temptation. He returned with the non-dual insight of the Upanishads — and spoke of the kingdom of Jah as something not in the future but present, not in heaven but within — The kingdom of Jah is within you. He returned with the Nath mastery over the elements — and walked on water, calmed storms, fed multitudes from almost nothing.

He returned to his people — to the lost sheep of Israel in Palestine — and brought them everything he had learned, filtered through the lens of the Hebrew covenant, expressed in the Aramaic language of his childhood, rooted in the Torah and the Psalms and the Prophets that he had known since birth. He was not a Hindu teacher. He was not a Buddhist teacher. He was not a Zoroastrian teacher. He was Yeshua of Nazareth, the Anointed One, the son of the living Jah. But he had learned from every stream of divine wisdom that existed on earth, and he had synthesised them all into something entirely new — the teaching of unconditional love, radical forgiveness, and the direct availability of the kingdom of Jah to every human soul, of every race, in every condition, in every moment.

This is the fullness of the Christ. Not the diminished figure of institutional religion — locked in doctrine, confined to one tradition, available only through authorised mediators. But the cosmic Yeshua — Issa of the East, Isha Nath of the Nath tradition, Yuz Asaf of Kashmir, the Teacher of Righteousness of the Essenes, the fulfillment of the Vedas and the Torah and the Dhamma and the Zoroastrian fire — the one in whom all the rivers of divine wisdom meet and from whom flows the ocean of love that has no shore.
