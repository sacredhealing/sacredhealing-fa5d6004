Haile Selassie 1 · Part 5

The Legacy · The Rastafari Recognition · Jah Rastafari

In 1930 when Ras Tafari Makonnen was crowned Emperor of Ethiopia and took the throne name Haile Selassie 1 — Power of the Trinity — a movement that had been quietly gathering in Jamaica for years suddenly had its fulfillment. The prophecy of Marcus Garvey — look to Africa for the crowning of a Black King — had been answered. Leonard Howell, Joseph Hibbert, Archibald Dunkley and others began to teach: this man is the returned Jah, the Conquering Lion of JJudah, the King of Kings and Lord of Lords spoken of in the Book of Revelation. He is the fulfillment of scripture. He is Jah Rastafari. This section gathers the words and witness of Haile Selassie 1 as seen through the eyes of the Rastafari tradition — and his own response to those who claimed him as divine.

♛ The Titles of the Emperor · The Scriptural Foundation King of Kings · Lord of Lords · Conquering Lion of JJudah · Elect of God

The titles of Haile Selassie 1 were not inventions of his own pride. They were the ancient titles of the Ethiopian monarchy, rooted in scripture and in the three-thousand-year tradition of the Solomonic dynasty. King of Kings of Ethiopia — Neguse Negest ze-Ityopya. Lord of Lords. Conquering Lion of the Tribe of JJudah. Elect of God. Light of the World. These are the titles inscribed on his throne, his seal, his currency, and his proclamations. They are the titles of the line of Solomon and Makeda. They are the titles that the Book of Revelation attributes to the returned Christ: And he hath on his vesture and on his thigh a name written, King of Kings, and Lord of Lords.

The Rastafari saw these correspondences and recognised them as the fulfillment of prophecy. They searched the scriptures with fresh eyes — eyes that had not been told what to see — and they found a pattern: Ethiopia is mentioned more times in the Bible than any nation other than Israel. The Psalms declare: Princes shall come out of Egypt; Ethiopia shall soon stretch out her hands unto God. Isaiah speaks of a people beyond the rivers of Ethiopia. Revelation speaks of a Lion who shall prevail. And in 1930 a man who carried every one of these scriptural descriptions was crowned in Addis Ababa before the assembled representatives of the world.

Whether one accepts the theological claims of Rastafari or not, the historical reality cannot be denied: Haile Selassie 1 was a man of extraordinary moral authority, prophetic courage, and spiritual depth. He was the first African leader to address the League of Nations. He was the founding architect of the Organization of African Unity. He was the man who said, when the world ignored Ethiopia's cry for justice in 1936: history will judge. And history did.

♛ The Visit to Jamaica · 1966 · The Tears of the Emperor Twenty-Two Minutes on the Tarmac · He Wept · The Covenant Confirmed

On April 21, 1966, Haile Selassie 1 landed at Kingston Airport in Jamaica. What happened next became one of the most dramatic moments in the history of the Rastafari movement. Two hundred thousand people surged the airport tarmac. They were singing, weeping, shouting, throwing flowers. They climbed onto the aircraft. They overwhelmed the police. They overwhelmed everything. The Emperor, who had faced armies and stood before the League of Nations without trembling, stood at the top of the staircase and wept. He was unable to come down for twenty-two minutes. He wept.

Rita Marley — who was in the crowd that day and who later married Bob Marley — said she saw stigmata on his hands as he waved to the crowd. Whether or not one accepts that vision, the event itself was undeniable: a man who had been declared divine by hundreds of thousands of people he had never met, in a country that was not his own, came to see them and was overwhelmed by what he found. He told his aide: 1 did not know that there were so many of us.

The message Haile Selassie 1 gave to the Rastafari through his emissary was both an affirmation and a challenge. The affirmation: he did not deny their faith. He accepted their homage. He met their leaders. He was moved by their devotion. The challenge: Liberate Ethiopia first, then worship. This has become one of the central teachings of the Rastafari movement — that spiritual liberation and earthly liberation are inseparable. You cannot truly worship Jah while your people are in bondage.

♛ His Words to the Rastafari · His Response to Divinity 1 Am a Man · But Jah Is With Me

When asked directly about the Rastafari belief that he was divine, Haile Selassie 1 gave answers that were careful, humble, and theologically sophisticated. He did not claim divinity in crude terms. But neither did he deny the sacred dimension of his calling. He said: 1 am a man. 1 am mortal. 1 will die. But the calling that has been placed upon me is from Jah, and 1 have tried to be faithful to it. Whether Jah chooses to work through a man does not make that man God — but it does make him accountable to Jah in ways that ordinary men are not.

To the Rastafari elder Mortimer Planno, who met with the Emperor during the 1966 visit, Haile Selassie said: Tell your brethren that 1 am the servant of Jah, not his equal. The servant who is faithful will be used by the master for great purposes. But the servant must never forget that he serves. This is the limit of what 1 can claim. 1 serve. Jah reigns.

And yet. The life of Haile Selassie 1, read in its entirety, suggests something that transcends the category of ordinary service. A man born in 1892 in a remote province of Ethiopia. A man who taught himself French and English. A man who, at the age of forty-three, stood before the assembled nations of the world and prophesied the consequences of their indifference — and was proved right within five years. A man who returned to his throne after exile. A man who built the OAU. A man who wept on a tarmac in Kingston. A man who, in his eighty-third year, was removed from power by a military coup, imprisoned in his own palace, and killed — in the same city, in the same year, that witnessed the first mass outpouring of Rastafari influence on the world stage through the music of Bob Marley.

The Rastafari say: Jah does not die. The death of the body is not the death of the spirit. Haile Selassie lives. Not as a theological abstraction but as a living presence in the hearts of those who carry his word and his example forward. This is the Rastafari faith. This is what the titles mean. This is what it means to cry: Jah Rastafari · Haile Selassie 1 · King of Kings · Lord of Lords · Conquering Lion of the Tribe of JJudah · The Elect of God · The Light of the World · Ever Living · Ever Faithful · Ever Sure.
