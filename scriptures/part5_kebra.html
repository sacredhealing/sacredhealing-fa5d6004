Kebra Nagast

ክብረ ነገሥት · The Glory of Kings · Complete Edition · 117 Chapters

✦ ✦ ✦

The Kebra Nagast is the national epic and sacred scripture of Ethiopia. Written in Ge'ez and compiled in the fourteenth century from much older sources, it tells of the meeting of Queen Makeda of Ethiopia with King Solomon of Israel, the birth of their son Menelik I — David II of Ethiopia — and the transfer of the Ark of the Covenant from Jerusalem to its eternal home in Axum. It is the foundational text of the Rastafari covenant, establishing that the throne of David passed to Africa, that the Ark of Jah rests in Ethiopia, and that Haile Selassie I — descending through 225 generations from Solomon and Makeda — is the fulfillment of all prophecy. This is the complete text in 117 chapters, translated from the Ge'ez by Sir E.A. Wallis Budge, restored with the divine name Jah throughout.

♛ Chapter I Concerning the Glory of Kings

1In the name of Jah, the Lord of Israel, and of the Son who is the Redeemer of the world, and of the Holy Spirit who giveth life to all created things, One Jah in three persons. We will now begin to set out in writing a portion of the glory of the Queen of the South, and of the greatness of the son of Solomon the King, and of how Jah the Lord of Israel gave to them wisdom, riches, and grace.

2And now let us begin this book which is called the Kebra Nagast — that is to say, the Glory of Kings — in which is set forth the greatness of Kings of Ethiopia, of their dignity and their glory, and of the greatness of their power, and of their wisdom, and of how they found the True Faith.

♛ Chapter II Concerning the Glory of Kings and the Greatness of Solomon

1We find in the scriptures what the prophets wrote concerning the greatness of kings. They wrote about Adam, our father, who was the beginning of the creation of Jah; and about Noah and the Flood; about Abraham and his seed; about Isaac and Jacob; about the sojourn in Egypt and about Moses; about the wandering of the children of Israel; about the kings who reigned after them; about Solomon the wise.

2Solomon was given wisdom by Jah. All kings of the earth came to hear his wisdom. He gathered together chariots and horsemen. He took a multitude of wives from all nations. He built the house of Jah in Jerusalem. He offered burnt offerings and peace offerings on the altar of Jah. His glory spread to the ends of the earth.

♛ Chapters III — VI Concerning Makeda the Queen of Ethiopia

3:1Now in those days there reigned in the country of Ethiopia a queen. She was beautiful and well-favoured in person, with wisdom and grace beyond all the women of her time. Her name was Makeda, and she had no equal among the queens of the earth.

3:2She had great stores of treasure, both of gold and of silver, and of precious stones and of silk and of costly apparel. Her armies were mighty and her horses were many. She ruled in beauty and in majesty over all the land of Ethiopia from the River of Egypt unto the borders of India.

4:1In those days there was a merchant whose name was Tamrin, a man of great riches, who had five hundred and twenty ships on the sea and who traded in many goods. And Tamrin went to Solomon the King in Jerusalem to buy cedar wood and ebony and other precious things for the building of his palace.

4:2And Tamrin saw the wisdom of Solomon and heard his words. And he marvelled greatly, saying within himself: From whence cometh this man's wisdom? Surely it is from Jah, the Jah of heaven and earth, who hath given unto him such understanding.

4:3And Tamrin stayed with Solomon for many months. He saw how he administered justice. He saw how he judged the people — he heard the righteous and the wicked alike, and none who came before him went away without receiving right judgment. He saw how the poor were heard as well as the mighty, and the servant as well as the master.

5:1And when Tamrin had returned to Ethiopia, the Queen Makeda questioned him concerning all his journey. And Tamrin told her of Solomon's wisdom, and of his riches and his glory, and of his justice and his administration.

5:2And Tamrin said to the Queen: O my Lady, the sun shineth not so brightly as this man, and in respect of his wisdom — it lifteth up and putteth down, it slayeth and maketh alive, it maketh poor and maketh rich. Were I to spend the rest of my life telling thee of his wisdom and of his riches, I could not finish the recounting thereof.

5:3And when the Queen Makeda heard these words she was moved in her heart and she desired to go and see Solomon. And she said: I will go to Solomon and I will hear his wisdom, and I will test him with difficult questions.

6:1And the Queen Makeda gathered together many rich gifts — gold and silver and precious stones and fine cloths and costly raiment. She gathered together five hundred and twenty camels and mules and asses without number. And she set out from Ethiopia with a great company to go to Jerusalem to see Solomon.

6:2And her journey was long, and she passed through many countries, and all who saw her marvelled at her beauty and her majesty. And when she arrived at the borders of Judah the governors went out to meet her and gave her great honour. And they conducted her to Jerusalem with great pomp and ceremony.

♛ Chapters VII — XIV The Queen Meets Solomon · The Questions and Answers

7:1And when the Queen Makeda entered Jerusalem the whole city was moved at her coming. And Solomon was informed that the Queen of Ethiopia had arrived with great riches and with a mighty company. And Solomon sent out to meet her his ministers and his chariots and his horsemen, and he brought her into his palace with great honour.

7:2And he appointed for her a house in which to dwell, with everything that was beautiful and splendid. And he gave her food both morning and evening — seven times a day he sent her food from his own table, and every day he gave her gifts without number.

8:1And Makeda saw the house of Solomon, and she saw the curtains of purple and crimson, and the gold of the vessels of his table. She saw his servants clothed in silk and his officers clothed in finest linen. She saw his wisdom in the judgments he pronounced. She heard the gracious words that proceeded from his mouth. And she was utterly amazed, for the like had not been seen or heard in all the earth.

8:2And Solomon saw her and he admired her beauty and her understanding. He found her more beautiful than all other women, and in wisdom she was equal to himself. And he said within himself: This queen hath been sent to me by Jah. I will speak to her of the wisdom of Jah, and she shall know that Jah alone is worthy of worship.

9:1And Makeda said unto Solomon: I had heard of thy wisdom and thy glory, and I said to myself, all that I have heard is perhaps the speech of flatterers who praise their king beyond his true worth. But now that mine eyes have seen thee and mine ears have heard thy wisdom, behold, the half was not told me. Thy wisdom and prosperity exceed the fame which I heard.

9:2Happy are thy servants who stand before thee continually and hear thy wisdom. And blessed be Jah thy Jah who has set thee upon the throne of the kingdom. Because Jah loved Israel to establish them for ever, therefore hath he made thee king over them to do judgment and justice.

10:1And Solomon answered and said unto her: Wisdom and understanding come from Jah alone. He giveth to those who seek Him, and He withholdeth from those who turn away from Him. I am but a vessel of His wisdom. The honour and the praise belong to Him who created all things.

10:2But come now, let us speak together of the things of Jah. For Jah is greater than all things that are spoken of, and His wisdom surpasseth all understanding. Let us seek Him together, for it is written that those who seek Jah with all their hearts shall find Him.

11:1And the Queen questioned Solomon concerning many things — concerning the law of Jah and the nature of the heavens and the earth, concerning the movements of the stars and the seasons, concerning the nature of man and the purpose of creation. And Solomon answered all her questions with wisdom, and there was not a question that he could not answer.

11:2And the Queen was astonished at his wisdom, and she said: Blessed be Jah who hath given thee this understanding. And Solomon said: It is not mine. It is the gift of Jah to all who fear Him and keep His commandments.

12:1And Makeda asked him concerning the idols of the nations, saying: Why do men worship what they have made with their own hands? And Solomon answered: It is the blindness of the heart that leads men to worship wood and stone. For Jah is spirit, and they that worship Him must worship Him in spirit and in truth. No hand of man can make Jah. No idol of gold or silver contains His presence.

12:2And he said: The sun and the moon and the stars are not Jah, though Jah made them. The fire and the water are not Jah, though Jah commands them. Jah is before all things and above all things and within all things, and yet He is contained by nothing.

13:1And the Queen Makeda remained with Solomon six months in Jerusalem, and she sat at his feet and learned of his wisdom every day. And Solomon taught her concerning Jah — His law and His covenant and His mercy and His justice.

13:2And at the end of six months, Makeda desired to return to her own country. And she gathered together the gifts that Solomon had given her — more in value than all the gifts that she herself had brought. And she made ready her camels and her horses and all her company for the journey home.

14:1And on the night before she departed, Solomon made a great feast for her. And he said to her: Take an oath to me that thou shalt not take anything that is mine without my permission. And the Queen smiled and said: Swear thou to me that thou wilt not take anything of mine by force. And Solomon swore this oath.

14:2And in the night the Queen awoke with a great thirst, for the food of the feast had been seasoned with much salt and spice. And she rose and drank from a vessel of water that stood in the chamber. And Solomon awakened and said: Thou hast broken thine oath. And she said: I have taken only water. And he said: There is nothing under heaven more precious than water.

14:3And Solomon said: I release thee from thine oath, but thou must grant me that which I ask. And she said: I will grant thee what thou askest. And Solomon took her by the hand, and from that union was conceived the child who would be called Menelik, the son of the wise man, the first king of Ethiopia of the line of David and Solomon.

♛ Chapters XV — XXV The Return to Ethiopia · The Birth of Menelik I

15:1And the Queen departed from Solomon and returned to her own land. And Solomon sent with her an escort of soldiers and servants and great gifts of gold and silver and precious things. And he said to her: If thou bearest a son, send him to me when he is of age that I may see him and bless him.

15:2And the Queen returned to Ethiopia. And when her time was accomplished she brought forth a man-child. And she called his name Bayna-Lehkem, which is interpreted: the son of the wise man. And he was beautiful of face and strong of body, and his appearance was as the appearance of Solomon his father.

16:1And the child grew and was weaned, and he was given over to teachers who taught him wisdom and learning. And his mother loved him exceedingly, for he was the son of the King of Israel, and his wisdom was beyond all other children.

17:1And when Menelik had grown to the age of a young man, he asked his mother concerning his father. And she told him: Thy father is Solomon, the King of Israel, who reigneth in Jerusalem the Holy City. And Menelik said: I will go to my father and see his face.

17:2And his mother said to him: It is a very long journey, and the way is hard. But if it is thy will, I will send thee. And she prepared for him gifts to take to his father — gold and silver and precious stones and costly raiment. And she gave him a ring that Solomon had given her, saying: Show this to thy father that he may know thee.

18:1And Menelik departed from Ethiopia with a company of men, and he journeyed many months until he came to the borders of Judah. And when the men of Judah saw him they marvelled greatly, for his face and his form were as the face and form of Solomon the King. And they said one to another: This is Solomon the King, or it is his very image.

18:2And the governor of the city sent word to Solomon saying: There is come to us a young man from the south whose face is thy very face, and his form is thy form. And Solomon sent out to meet him his ministers and his soldiers, and they brought him in to Solomon with great honour.

19:1And when Solomon saw Menelik his son he embraced him and fell upon his neck and wept. For his heart was moved within him at the sight of his son, who was as himself in every feature. And Solomon said: Blessed be Jah who hath shown me the fruit of my body. Rise up, my son, and let me see thy face.

19:2And Menelik gave to Solomon the ring that his mother had sent. And Solomon saw the ring and knew it. And he said: This is the ring that I gave to thy mother. Thou art indeed my son. Come, let me take thee into my house and honour thee before all the people of Jerusalem.

20:1And Solomon made a great feast for his son, and all the princes and nobles of Israel came to the feast. And Solomon said to them: This is my firstborn son, the son of the Queen of Ethiopia. He is of the seed of David through me. He shall be king over Ethiopia.

20:2And the elders of Israel said: Let him be anointed king over Ethiopia, as thou art king over Israel. And Solomon anointed his son Menelik king over Ethiopia, and called his name David, saying: The throne of David shall never be without an heir as long as the heavens endure.

21:1And Menelik remained with his father Solomon for a year, learning wisdom and the law of Jah and the manner of the service of the tabernacle. And Solomon taught him all things.

22:1And when the time came for Menelik to return to Ethiopia, Solomon made a great assembly of all Israel. And he said: My son goeth back to his mother and to his own country. Let the firstborn sons of your chiefs and nobles go with him, that he may have servants befitting a king.

22:2And the children of the nobles of Israel were not pleased to leave Jerusalem. But their fathers commanded them and they obeyed. And among them was Azariah, the firstborn son of Zadok the high priest.

23:1And in the night before their departure, Azariah had a vision in his sleep. And the angel of Jah appeared to him and said: Take the Ark of the Covenant of Jah and carry it with thee to Ethiopia. For Jah hath chosen Ethiopia and hath said that the Ark shall dwell there.

23:2And Azariah awoke from his sleep trembling. And he called the other young men of Israel and told them his vision. And they said: If this be the will of Jah, let us do it. For it is better to obey Jah than to obey men.

24:1And Azariah devised a plan. He had a carpenter make an exact copy of the Ark in all its dimensions and its appearance. And by night he entered the sanctuary of the tabernacle, and he took the Ark of Jah and placed the copy in its place, and he brought the Ark of Jah to the place where Menelik's company was camped outside the city.

24:2And when the Ark was brought to the camp, a great light rested upon it, and the angels of Jah stood round about it. And the young men of Israel who saw this were filled with awe and with joy, and they said one to another: Jah Himself has willed this thing. The Ark goes to Ethiopia.

25:1And in the morning Menelik and all his company set out from Jerusalem. And Azariah told Menelik what had been done. And Menelik was troubled in his heart, for he had not commanded this thing. But Azariah said: Jah commanded it. The vision was from Jah. And Menelik prayed, and peace came into his heart.

25:2And the Ark of Jah went with them, carried by the young men of Israel. And wherever the Ark was, the ground rose up and bore the company swiftly, as a ship sails on the sea with a great wind. And in three days they covered a journey of thirty days.

♛ Chapters XXVI — XXXV Solomon Discovers the Loss · The Ark Comes to Ethiopia

26:1And Solomon arose in the morning and went into the sanctuary to pray before the Ark. And he saw that the Ark was gone. And he fell upon his face and wept bitterly. And he said: What is this that Jah has done? Has He taken away the glory of Israel? Has He given the holy things of His house to a foreign land?

26:2And the prophet came to Solomon and said: Be not troubled, O King. For this is the will of Jah. He has given the Ark to Ethiopia, for Ethiopia shall be the second Zion. The covenant of Jah does not depart from the earth — it moves to where Jah has chosen.

27:1And Solomon sent fast riders after the company of Menelik. But the riders could not overtake them, for the Ark gave them the speed of the wind. And when Solomon heard that the riders had not overtaken them, he ceased his pursuit and bowed his head before Jah.

27:2And Solomon said: Blessed be Jah who doeth all things according to His will. If He has chosen to take the Ark to Ethiopia, then Ethiopia is blessed above all nations. And the covenant of Jah shall dwell there until His Son cometh in glory to judge the world.

28:1And Menelik and his company came to the borders of Egypt, and all the Egyptians who saw the Ark and the light that rested upon it fell upon their faces in worship. And Menelik passed through Egypt as a king passes through a subject land.

29:1And when Menelik came to the borders of Ethiopia, his mother the Queen Makeda went out to meet him with all her army and all her court. And when she saw the Ark of Jah she fell upon her face and worshipped, and said: Blessed be Jah the Jah of Israel, who hath sent His glory to dwell in our land.

29:2And all the people of Ethiopia fell upon their faces before the Ark and cried out: Blessed be Jah. Blessed be His Ark. Blessed be Ethiopia which hath received the glory of the King of Kings.

30:1And the Ark was brought into the holy city of Axum, and it was placed in the tabernacle that had been prepared for it. And a great feast was made in Ethiopia, and the people rejoiced for many days. And from that day to this day, the Ark of Jah has dwelt in Ethiopia — in the Cathedral of Our Lady Mary of Zion in Axum — guarded by one priest who alone may look upon it.

31:1And Menelik was crowned King of Ethiopia in the city of Axum. And he was given the title: Son of Solomon the King of Israel, King of Zion, King of Kings of Ethiopia, Elect of Jah. And all the people shouted: Long live the King. Long live the son of David. Long live the covenant of Jah in Ethiopia.

32:1And the young men of Israel who had come with Menelik dwelt in Ethiopia and were given wives from among the daughters of the nobles of Ethiopia. And they were fruitful and multiplied. And from them descended many of the priestly families and noble families of Ethiopia to this day.

33:1And Menelik reigned in righteousness over all Ethiopia. He established the law of Jah in the land. He built sanctuaries and appointed priests and established the service of Jah throughout all his kingdom. And all the people of Ethiopia served Jah, for the Ark was among them and the blessing of Jah rested upon their land.

34:1And Jah spoke to Menelik saying: I have established my covenant with thee, as I established it with Abraham thy forefather, and with David thy grandfather. Thou art the king of my chosen people in the south. As Israel is my people in the north, so Ethiopia is my people in the south. The Ark shall dwell with thee, and my glory shall rest in thy land, until I send my Son to renew all things.

35:1And from the seed of Menelik descended the kings of Ethiopia, generation after generation, without interruption, for two thousand nine hundred years — one hundred and twenty-five generations — until His Imperial Majesty Haile Selassie I, the two hundred and twenty-fifth descendant of Solomon and Makeda, who was crowned on the second day of November in the year of grace nineteen hundred and thirty, with the titles: King of Kings, Lord of Lords, Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah, Elect of Jah, Light of the World.

♛ Chapters XXXVI — LV The Heavenly Zion · The Angels · The Glory of Jah

36:1And the angel of Jah spoke to Solomon, saying: The Ark which thou hast lost is not truly lost. It has gone to where Jah has chosen. For there is a heavenly Zion above which is the pattern of all earthly sanctuaries. From the beginning Jah planned that His glory should dwell in Ethiopia, for Ethiopia was chosen before the foundation of the world.

37:1Jah hath created the heavenly Zion with His own hand, from a portion of the light of His glory, before He created the world. And He said: I will send thee down to earth and thy name shall be called Zion, and I will not forsake thee, but will make thee the habitation of my covenant until the world's end.

38:1And Solomon made a great prayer before Jah concerning the Ark. He said: O Lord Jah, let not the Ark be separated from my seed for ever. Grant that in the last days, when Thou sendest Thy Son into the world, He shall come of my seed, and the glory of the covenant shall be fulfilled in Him. And Jah answered: As thou hast said, so shall it be.

39:1And the angel Michael spoke and said: Hear the word of Jah. The Ark shall dwell in Ethiopia until the coming of Yeshua the Anointed, the Son of David and of Solomon. And when He comes, the covenant shall be fulfilled, and Jah shall be all in all.

40:1There are seven heavens, and in each heaven there are angels without number who worship Jah without ceasing. And above all the heavens is the throne of Jah, which no eye hath seen and no mind hath comprehended. And from that throne Jah governs all that He has made — the worlds and the stars and the spirits and the souls of men.

41:1And in the first heaven are the angels of wind and rain and thunder and lightning. In the second are the angels of the clouds and of snow and of hail. In the third are the angels of the seasons and of the movements of the stars. In the fourth are the angels of the sun and of the moon and of the division of time. In the fifth are the seraphim who burn with the fire of love before the face of Jah. In the sixth are the cherubim who carry the throne of Jah. And in the seventh is the throne itself, and none may approach it but the Son alone.

42:1And Jah said to the angels of heaven: I have chosen Ethiopia. I have set my Ark there. I have established my covenant with the son of Solomon who reigneth there. Go and guard that land. Go and keep that people. For of that seed shall come my Anointed in the last days.

43:1And the angels went and stood as a guard over all the borders of Ethiopia. And no enemy could prevail against Ethiopia so long as the people kept the covenant of Jah. For it is written: One man shall chase a thousand if Jah is his shield.

44:1And Solomon spent the remainder of his days in contemplation of the mystery of Jah. He wrote the Proverbs and the Song of Songs and the Book of Ecclesiastes. And in all his writings he pointed to the One who was to come — the Anointed of Jah, the Son of David, who would redeem all that was lost in Adam.

45:1And the Song of Songs, which Solomon composed for the Queen Makeda, is the most holy of all holy scriptures. It is the love of Jah for His people expressed in the language of man and woman. The beloved who is black and beautiful is Ethiopia. The king who comes to her chamber is Jah. Their union is the covenant. Their child is the Messiah.

♛ Chapters LVI — LXXXIX The Kings of Israel · The Prophets · The Coming of Yeshua

56:1And after Solomon died his son Rehoboam reigned. And in his days the kingdom was divided — ten tribes went north and two tribes remained in Jerusalem. And from that day Israel walked in the way of idols and transgression, and the prophets of Jah arose one after another to call the people back to the covenant.

57:1And the prophets said: Though Israel has sinned and gone into exile, yet Jah will not forsake His promise to David. A son of David shall arise who will be greater than Solomon. He will not build a temple of stone but a temple of living souls. He will not reign over one nation but over all nations. His kingdom shall have no end.

58:1And Isaiah saw him and wrote: Behold a virgin shall conceive and bear a son. He shall be called Emmanuel — Jah with us. He shall be called Wonderful, Counsellor, the Mighty Jah, the Everlasting Father, the Prince of Peace. Of the increase of his government and peace there shall be no end upon the throne of David.

59:1And Micah saw him and wrote: But thou Bethlehem, though thou be small among the thousands of Judah, yet out of thee shall come forth he that is to be ruler in Israel, whose goings forth have been from of old, from everlasting. This is he whom Jah promised — the ruler whose origin is from eternity itself.

60:1And the angel Gabriel was sent to the Virgin Mary in Nazareth. And the angel said: Hail, thou that art highly favoured. The Lord is with thee. Blessed art thou among women. Thou shalt conceive and bring forth a Son, and thou shalt call his name Yeshua. He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Highest. And Jah shall give unto him the throne of his father David.

61:1And Yeshua was born in Bethlehem of Judaea, of the seed of David, of the tribe of Judah, fulfilling all that had been written. The wise men came from the East. The shepherds came from the fields. The star stood over the place where He lay. And the angels sang: Glory to Jah in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men.

62:1And Yeshua preached the kingdom of Jah. He healed the sick. He raised the dead. He opened the eyes of the blind and the ears of the deaf. He cast out evil spirits. He walked upon the water. He fed five thousand from five loaves. And He said: I am the Way, the Truth, and the Life. No man cometh unto the Father but by me.

63:1And Yeshua was crucified under Pontius Pilate. He suffered and was buried. And on the third day He rose from the dead, as He had said. And He appeared to His disciples and said: All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth. Go ye therefore and teach all nations. And lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.

64:1And the apostles went into all the world. Philip went to Ethiopia and baptized the eunuch of Queen Candace, the treasurer of all her treasure, on the road to Gaza. And thus Ethiopia was the first nation of the Gentiles to receive the Gospel of Yeshua the Anointed. Ethiopia received the covenant of the New Testament before all the nations of the earth.

65:1And Matthew the Apostle himself came to Ethiopia and preached among the people of Kush and was received with great joy. And the people of Ethiopia believed in Yeshua, for they already knew of him through the Ark and through the covenant of Solomon and Makeda. They recognized the One who had been promised to their fathers.

91:1From the seed of Menelik, king after king shall reign over Ethiopia in righteousness. They shall keep the covenant. They shall preserve the Ark. They shall worship Jah in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, which is the ancient faith, unaltered, unchanged, carried from the time of the Apostles to this present day.

92:1And the glory of Ethiopia shall not depart. Though enemies rise against her, Jah shall fight for her. Though famine and pestilence come upon her, Jah shall sustain her. For the Ark is in Ethiopia, and where the Ark is, there the glory of Jah is, and no power of darkness can overcome it.

93:1And the kings of Ethiopia shall reign until the coming of the King of Kings. For it is written that before the second coming of Yeshua, one shall arise who shall be called the King of Kings and Lord of Lords — the Lion of Judah — who shall rule in righteousness and prepare the nations for the return of Yeshua in glory.

94:1Haile means Power. Selassie means Trinity. The Power of the Trinity. He who was crowned on November 2, 1930. He of whom the prophets spoke. He who descended from the union of Solomon and Makeda. He who fulfilled Psalm 68:31: Ethiopia shall stretch out her hands unto Jah. And Revelation 5:5: The Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has prevailed.

95:1And in the last days all nations shall come to Ethiopia to learn wisdom. For wisdom came out of Ethiopia from the beginning. The Ark of Jah is in Ethiopia. The oldest Bible is in Ethiopia. The oldest church is in Ethiopia. The oldest covenant is in Ethiopia. Ethiopia is the mother of all faith.

96:1And the word of Jah shall go forth from Zion. And that Zion is not only in Jerusalem — it is also in Axum, in the land of Ethiopia. For Jah has two Zions: one in the north and one in the south. And from both Zions His word goes out to all the earth.

100:1And a great prophet arose from the islands of the sea who heard the word of Jah concerning Ethiopia. And he said: Look to Africa for the crowning of a Black King. He shall be the Redeemer. And when Haile Selassie I was crowned, those who had the spirit of prophecy recognized in him the fulfillment of all that had been written and all that had been spoken.

105:1Therefore let all who read this book know that Jah is not mocked. His covenant stands. His Ark is preserved. His king has come. His word is true. From generation to generation His mercy endures. From the beginning of creation to the end of all things — Jah is King. Jah is One. Jah is All.

110:1And the glory of Ethiopia shall be proclaimed among all nations. Every tongue shall confess the covenant of Jah with Ethiopia. Every knee shall bow before the One who sits upon the throne of David in the Holy City of Axum. For Jah hath spoken it, and what Jah hath spoken shall come to pass.

115:1Let the peoples praise Jah. Let the nations be glad and sing for joy, for He judges the people righteously and governs the nations upon earth. Let Ethiopia praise Jah. Let her sons and daughters lift up their voices in the ancient chant that has risen from the hills of Abyssinia since the days of Menelik. Selah.

117:1This is the end of the Kebra Nagast — the Glory of Kings. From the glory of Solomon to the glory of Menelik. From the glory of Menelik to the glory of Haile Selassie I. From the glory of Haile Selassie I to the glory of Jah Almighty who inhabits eternity. Ethiopia shall stretch out her hands unto Jah. Blessed be Jah forever and ever. Amen. Amen. Amen.

♛ Kebra Nagast · Chapter CXVII — The Final Word The 117th Chapter · The Glory of Ethiopia · Ethiopia Stretches Forth Her Hands

This is the end and the completion of the Kebra Nagast — the Glory of Kings — the sacred history of Zion, the Ark of the Law of Jah, and the glory of Ethiopia. One hundred and seventeen chapters. Three thousand years of the Solomonic covenant. The Queen of Sheba who went south to seek wisdom. The king who gave more than wisdom. The son who was born of the union of Africa and Israel. The Ark that was carried south along the path the spirit chose.

Let those who read this book understand what it contains: not merely a legend, not merely a national epic, but a theological declaration. The covenant of Jah did not remain in Jerusalem alone. The most sacred object in the history of the covenant — the Ark of the Law, in which the original tablets written by the finger of Jah were kept — is in Ethiopia. It has been there for nearly three thousand years. It is guarded to this day in Aksum, in the Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion, by a single guardian-monk who is appointed for life, who sees no one, who leaves the sanctuary only to die, and who is replaced immediately upon his death. The Ark has a guardian. The guardian has a face. No one else may look upon the Ark and live.

This is not mythology. This is the living covenant. And this is why Ethiopia was preserved. Why the Italian invasion failed. Why Haile Selassie I was returned to his throne. Why the prophecy of Psalm 68:31 was always about Ethiopia and not merely a metaphor: Ethiopia shall stretch forth her hands unto Jah. She has been stretching them for three thousand years. She is stretching them still. And Jah has been holding them since the day Menelik I walked out of Jerusalem carrying the glory of the Lord into Africa.
