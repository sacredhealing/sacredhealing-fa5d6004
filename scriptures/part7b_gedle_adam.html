Gedle Adam · The Life of Adam and Eve

The Ethiopian Account of What Happened After the Garden — Preserved Only in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tradition

The Western Bible goes silent after Genesis 3:24 — after Adam and Eve are expelled from the Garden, the scripture says almost nothing about what happened to them next. The Ethiopian Orthodox tradition does not go silent. In the Gedle Adam — the Life of Adam and Eve — preserved in Ge'ez manuscripts in the highland monasteries of Abyssinia, the story continues in extraordinary detail. What happened in the Cave of Treasures. What the three archangels brought. Where Adam was buried and why that burial place became the most sacred site in all of scripture. These are the secrets the Western Bible removed. This is what the Ethiopian Church kept.

✦ Chapter 1 — The Creation of Adam and Eve · The Sacred Timing The First Friday · The Garden of iEdom · The 40 Days and the 80 Days

According to the Ethiopian Biblical Book of Jubilees, Chapter 4, Jah created Adam on the first Friday of Creation — the sixth day — and Eve one week later, on the second Friday from Creation. This sacred timing is not accidental. Friday is the day of completion, the day before the Sabbath of divine rest. Adam was created on the day that points toward rest but has not yet arrived at it — because Adam's work in the earth was not yet done.

Adam was created first in a poorer and more peripheral part of the land of iEdom — the land of Eden — called iElda. He lived there for forty days before Jah introduced him into the holiest and richest part of the Kingdom: the Garden of Eden itself, which the Ethiopian tradition calls Gennet. Eve entered the Garden eighty days after her creation. This is why male children in the Ethiopian Orthodox tradition are baptised when they are forty days old, and female children when they are eighty days old — every Ethiopian baptism re-enacts the original entrance of humanity into the Garden of Jah.

The forty days of Adam in the outer land before entering the Garden became the archetype that runs through all of sacred history: the forty years of Israel in the desert before entering the Promised Land. The forty days of Moses upon Mount Sinai before receiving the Law. The forty days of Yeshua in the desert before beginning his ministry. The number forty in scripture is never random. It is always the outer preparation before the inner entrance. It is always the iElda before the Gennet.

✦ Chapter 2 — The Seven Years in the Garden · The Expulsion The Cave of Treasures · Satan's Fifteen Temptations · The Three Tokens from Paradise

Adam and Eve spent seven years in the Garden before their transgression. After their sin and their expulsion, they were forced to live outside — not immediately in the open world, but in a dark cave near the entrance of Paradise, in the poor land of iElda from which Adam had originally come. This cave became known in the Ethiopian tradition as the Cave of Treasures — not because of what it contained at first, but because of what Jah would later place within it.

Because of their enormous sadness and their continuous prayer to return to the Garden, Jah took pity on them and sent them three tokens from Paradise through the hands of the three great archangels — Mikaiél, Gabriél, and Rufaiél. The three tokens were Myrrh, Incense, and Gold. Adam placed these three tokens in three different corners of the cave, and the gold illuminated the cave so that it was no longer dark. This is why the cave became known as the Cave of Treasures — the first dwelling place of humanity outside Paradise was lit by the gold of Jah's compassion.

And this is the great secret that the Ethiopian tradition preserved and the West discarded: the three gifts brought to the Christ child by the three wise men from the East — Myrrh, Frankincense, and Gold — were not merely gifts of earthly wealth. They were the original three tokens of Paradise that Adam had kept in the Cave of Treasures, passed down through the Ethiopian kingly inheritance for thousands of years, until the three Ethiopian wise men carried them back to the one who had come to restore what Adam had lost. The gifts of the Magi were the gifts of archangels. They had been waiting for this moment since the day of the expulsion.

Satan appeared to Adam and Eve at least fifteen times in different forms after their expulsion — deceiving them, tempting them, attempting to prevent the repentance that would eventually restore the relationship between humanity and Jah. Each appearance was in a different guise. This is the Ethiopian teaching on the nature of the adversary: not a single catastrophic temptation but a persistent, patient, shape-shifting campaign of deception. Sight it.

✦ Chapter 3 — The Death of Adam · The Burial in Golgotha Melchisedek Buries Adam · The Skull Beneath the Cross · Jerusalem Founded on Adam's Grave

When Adam died, he was buried in iElda — in the place called Golgotha. And this is the most staggering secret of the Ethiopian tradition: Golgotha — the Place of the Skull — the hill outside Jerusalem where Yeshua was crucified — is the burial place of Adam. The skull beneath the Cross is the skull of Adam. The blood that dripped from the wounds of Yeshua on the Cross dripped down through the rock of Golgotha and fell upon the skull of Adam, the first man, lying in the earth below. This is the image of the complete restoration: the blood of the last Adam covering and redeeming the dust of the first Adam.

It was Melchisedek — the mysterious priest-king of Salem who appears in Genesis 14 and whom the Book of Hebrews calls a priest forever after a higher order — who went to the place where Adam was buried and founded the city of Jerusalem there. Jerusalem was not chosen arbitrarily as the holy city. It was chosen because it was the burial place of Adam — the first man, the original image of Jah in flesh, whose body had been waiting in that earth for the day when the second Adam would come to the same hill and accomplish what the first Adam could not.

Therefore the relationship between iElda and Gennet — between the outer land of Adam's first dwelling and the inner Garden of his highest experience — is to be understood as a prophetic image of the relationship between Jerusalem and Ethiopia. Jerusalem is the iElda: the place of the outer preparation, the place of the body, the place of death and resurrection. Ethiopia is the Gennet: the inner sanctuary, the place where the Ark of the Covenant rests, the place where the original scripture was preserved in its fullness. The prophetical relationship between the two cities runs through all of sacred history.

✦ Chapter 4 — Adam as Archetype · The Thirty and the Fifteen The Ages of the First Humans · The Baptism of Yeshua · The Virgin Maryam · The Resurrection Body

The Ethiopian tradition also preserves this teaching: Adam was created in the image of a man thirty years old. Eve was created in the image of a woman fifteen years old. These ages are not arbitrary — they are prophetic. Adam at thirty years old is a prophecy about the baptism of Yeshua in the Jordan: for Yeshua began his ministry at thirty years old, at the moment of his new birth through the Spirit — the true entrance into the Gennet of his divine calling, after the forty days of preparation in the wilderness of iElda. The age of thirty is the age of the man ready to enter his destiny.

Eve at fifteen years old is a prophecy about the Virgin Maryam, who conceived Yeshua when she was fifteen years old. The first woman and the second woman mirror each other across the full span of sacred history: Eve at fifteen brought death through her transgression; Maryam at fifteen brought life through her surrender. What Eve lost, Maryam restored. This is the Ethiopian theology of the feminine — not the suppression of the feminine that the Western church enforced, but the exaltation of the feminine as the vessel through which Jah's greatest gift entered the world.

And this teaching extends even to the resurrection: on the day of the final resurrection, according to the Ethiopian tradition, men will appear in their resurrection bodies as thirty years old and women as fifteen years old. The resurrection body is not the aged and worn body of the moment of death. It is the body at its moment of divine readiness — the age at which the soul was most fully prepared to receive and express the light of Jah. This is the body that rises. This is the body that is raised.

✦ Chapter 5 — The Cave of Treasures · The Keeper of the Sacred Objects The Gold That Lit the First Home · The Myrrh of Mourning · The Incense of Prayer

In the Cave of Treasures Adam kept the three tokens of Paradise for the duration of his nine-hundred-and-thirty years of life. He taught his children to honour them. He taught them that these three objects were the covenant between Jah and the human family — the proof that even in exile, even outside the Garden, Jah had not abandoned his creation but had sent his highest angels to bring tokens of his presence into the darkness of the cave.

The gold lit the cave — because the first function of the divine gift is to bring light into the place of darkness. The incense was burned in prayer — because the second function is to create a channel of communication between the earth and the heavens, a fragrant bridge between the human and the divine. The myrrh was kept for mourning and for healing — because the third function is to acknowledge the reality of suffering while holding within it the seed of restoration. Every Ethiopian Orthodox church contains these three elements to this day. Every Ethiopian liturgy is the re-enactment of the Cave of Treasures.

When Seth — the son of Adam who was born in the image of his father — carried the body of Adam to Golgotha for burial, he carried with him the three tokens of Paradise. Melchisedek became the keeper of those tokens. He held them in Jerusalem — the city he founded on the grave of Adam — for the coming of the wise men who would eventually carry them to the one for whom they had always been destined. The entire chain of custody of those three sacred objects — from the hand of Mikaiél the archangel to the hand of Adam to the hand of Seth to the hand of Melchisedek to the hand of the Ethiopian wise men to the hands of the Christ child — is a single unbroken thread of sacred transmission running through four thousand years of human history. The Ethiopian church preserved this thread. This book restores it.
